In pure physics and classical mechanics, **"capstone" and "keystone" are not types of pinions**, nor is a pinion defined by those terms.

However, your phrase maps perfectly to a specific piece of industrial engineering equipment. You are likely thinking of a **Keystone Pneumatic Actuator**, which utilizes a **double rack and pinion** mechanism.

Here is how these distinct physics, mechanical, and architectural concepts break down and interact:

### 1. The Industrial Link: Keystone Rack and Pinion

In mechanical control systems, **Keystone** is a major brand name of industrial valve actuators. 

![](../assets/407515d4c6d5b98c845c.jpg)Heap & Partners +1

* These devices use a **double rack and pinion** system to convert fluid pressure into rotational force.
* In this mechanical setup, linear pistons (racks) push against a central circular gear (the **pinion**) to open and close heavy factory valves. 

  ![](../assets/859bf173345d976733d6.png)Valve & Process Solutions +1

### 2. The Physics Definition of a Pinion

In physics and kinematics, a **pinion** is simply a circular gear. It is most famously studied in a **rack and pinion** mechanism, which converts rotational motion into linear motion (or vice versa). 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Beginning Engineers +1

* **Torque (τ)** applied to the pinion generates a linear **Force (F)** along the rack, calculated as τ = F × r (where r is the radius of the pinion pitch circle).
* This physics principle is what powers automotive steering wheels, stairlifts, and heavy industrial actuators. 

  ![](../assets/9118d08ae54e9cd4bf54.png)MathWorks +3

### 3. The Architecture Connection: Keystone & Capstone

"Keystone" and "capstone" belong to the physics of **statics and structural masonry**:

* **Keystone:** The central, wedge-shaped stone at the peak of an arch. In static physics, it is the most critical element because it redirects the downward force of gravity outward into lateral compressive force, locking the arch together so it bears weight without collapsing.
* **Capstone:** A flat protective stone placed on top of a wall structure. 

  ![](../assets/b3ec731e3b1392e72380.png)Logos Community +3
