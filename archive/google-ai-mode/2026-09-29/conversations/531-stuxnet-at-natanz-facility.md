## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

In 2010, **Iran's Natanz uranium enrichment facility** was infected with **Stuxnet**, a highly sophisticated computer worm widely believed to be a joint U.S.-Israeli intelligence operation. Because the nuclear facility's industrial control networks were "air-gapped"—completely physically isolated from the internet—the malware could not be delivered remotely. Instead, it was introduced using a standard **USB flash drive**. 

![](../assets/b080eef4f5784d42e035.png)Shieldworkz +5

---

### The Attack Vector: Removable Media

* **The Delivery:** Intelligence reports indicate that an insider—likely a recruited Iranian double agent or an unsuspecting outside contractor—inserted an infected USB thumb drive directly into a facility computer. 

  ![](../assets/dee259125574b6fa4f3b.png)IEEE Spectrum +5
* **The Propagation:** Once plugged into a single Windows machine, Stuxnet automatically executed by exploiting zero-day security vulnerabilities. It then silently spread across local networks and hopped onto other USB drives as employees shared files. 

  ![](../assets/28acff153d917ec6f458.png)CBS News +1

### The Impact

* **Targeted Sabotage:** The virus specifically looked for Siemens programmable logic controllers (PLCs) regulating the facility’s supersonic gas centrifuges. 

  ![](../assets/6ab30708800a4f99bbfa.png)WIRED
* **Physical Destruction:** Stuxnet forced the centrifuges to spin at dangerously high speeds and then drastically slow down, causing them to physically tear themselves apart. 

  ![](../assets/dee259125574b6fa4f3b.png)IEEE Spectrum +1
* **Deception:** While ruining the hardware, the virus intercepted the facility's monitoring systems to feed fake, normal-looking data to the control room, leaving operators completely unaware of the damage until it was too late. 

  ![](../assets/dee259125574b6fa4f3b.png)IEEE Spectrum

Ultimately, this single USB drive compromised the facility, **destroying roughly 1,000 centrifuges** and temporarily crippling Iran’s nuclear program. It is widely recognized as the world's first digital weapon used to cause intentional physical destruction to critical real-world infrastructure.
