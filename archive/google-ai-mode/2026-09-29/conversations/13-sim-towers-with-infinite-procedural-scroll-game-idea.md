### 🏢 The Core Concept: 'Stratosphere'

Instead of building on a fixed city lot, you build a single, ever-growing mega-structure that pushes infinitely upward. As the tower rises, the map procedurally generates new ambient challenges, resources, and structural mechanics. 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Retro Tech Dreams

* **The Infinite Elevator Dilemma:** In the classic game, elevators couldn't exceed 30 floors. Here, that restriction becomes the primary gameplay mechanic. You must build intricate networks of regular shafts, Express Sky-Lobbies, pneumatic tubes, and high-speed mag-lev lifts to keep tenants from abandoning the upper stratums. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Retro Tech Dreams
* **Procedural Biomes:** The higher you go, the environment actively changes:
  + *Floors 1–50 (The City Hub):* Standard offices, retail shops, and apartments.
  + *Floors 51–200 (The Cloud Layer):* High moisture, lightning strike risks, and wind shear that requires structural counter-weights.
  + *Floors 201–500 (The Low Orbit):* Low gravity (affecting how fast tenants walk), solar radiation risks, and the need to build enclosed life-support sectors.
  + *Floors 501+ (The Void):* True sci-fi territory requiring cosmic mining bays, asteroid shields, and bio-domes. 

    ![](../assets/6f3ef4d1f89b40615d5f.jpg)towersim.app

### ⚙️ Procedural Generation & Logistics Mechanics

Because the game scrolls infinitely, a standard manual placement loop would become exhausting. The game addresses this by introducing structural blueprints and semi-automated management systems: 

![](../assets/b8198132047e1c5b1c0d.png)Reddit

| System | Mechanic | Impact on Gameplay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Procedural Zoning** | You lay out structural "skeletons." The AI automatically populates local shops and offices based on the nearby demographic demand. | Eases macro-management fatigue as the tower reaches thousands of stories. |
| **Atmospheric Physics** | Upper floors sway due to wind. You must clear out structural space to build giant pendulums (Tuned Mass Dampers) to prevent structural collapse. | Prevents the tower from being a simple, repetitive block of rooms. |
| **Grid Shifts** | Every few hundred floors, the foundation footprint narrows or shifts based on the procedural terrain or skyway anchor points. | Forces you to adapt your room layouts to unexpected architectural shapes. |

### 🕹️ Game Loop & Progression Shifts

#### 1. The Micro-Society Loop

Your tower generates its own internal economy. Raw materials are gathered at the base or harvested from passing celestial bodies at the top. Workers commute between residential zones and workspaces. If an elevator delay takes too long, tenants get stressed, lower their rent, or dynamically pack up and leave. 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Hungry Goriya +2

#### 2. The Rogue-lite Skyscraper Twist

As you push higher, you trigger sudden "Structural Checkpoints." If a disaster strikes—like a massive lightning storm or a meteor strike—lower sections might sustain permanent damage. Rather than a game over, you have to choose whether to salvage resources and build a new structural foundation directly on top of the ruined lower floors. 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Retro Tech Dreams

#### 3. Macro Visuals

To solve the classic interface issue where you can't view the entire skyscraper at a glance, the game utilizes a seamless semantic zoom. Zoomed in, you see individual office workers eating at fast-food joints. Zoomed out, the view transforms into a sleek, glowing neon schematic vector tracking the flow of thousands of tiny elevator cars moving up and down an endless column.
