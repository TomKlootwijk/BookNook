### 🩸 Biological Capillaries

In human anatomy and physiology, capillaries are microscopic microvessels measuring just **5 to 10 micrometres in diameter**. They are so narrow that red blood cells must often squeeze through them in a single-file line. 

![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia

* **The Connection:** They form vast networks called capillary beds that connect the smallest arterial branches (**arterioles**) to the smallest venous branches (**venules**). 

  ![](../assets/b473770684cd9386734e.png)ScienceDirect.com +1
* **The Structure:** Their walls are composed of a single layer of simple squamous endothelial cells (the *tunica intima*) surrounded by a thin basement membrane. This ultra-thin barrier allows for efficient diffusion. 

  ![](../assets/163fed46ffb38fb00fb0.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +3
* **The Function:** Capillaries are the primary site of **substance exchange**. They drop off oxygen and essential nutrients to surrounding tissues while absorbing carbon dioxide and metabolic waste products like urea and lactic acid to be filtered out of the body. 

  ![](../assets/f2ee80ab5a884767fef3.png)Cleveland Clinic +1

#### Types of Blood Capillaries

The body utilizes three distinct variations depending on the level of permeability required by specific organs: 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·ThePenguinProf +1

| Capillary Type | Structural Features | Key Locations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Continuous** | Interlocking tight junctions; tightly sealed walls with no pores. | Nervous system (blood-brain barrier), lungs, fat, and muscle. |
| **Fenestrated** | Contains small pores (*fenestrae*) allowing faster molecule filtration. | Kidneys, small intestine, and endocrine glands. |
| **Sinusoidal** | Large structural gaps and incomplete basement membranes for maximum permeability. | Liver, spleen, and bone marrow. |

### 🧪 Physical Capillaries & Capillary Action

In physics and engineering, a capillary is any tube with an incredibly small internal diameter. These micro-tubes exhibit a phenomenon known as **capillary action**, which is the ability of a liquid to flow upward through narrow spaces against the force of gravity. 

![](../assets/56ae54a7f340c8ec61e4.png)USGS (.gov) +3

* **The Mechanics:** This movement is dictated by the relationship between **adhesive forces** (the fluid's attraction to the tube wall) and **cohesive forces** (the intermolecular attraction holding the fluid molecules together). 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·DestructiveCreativity +1
* **The Result:** When adhesion is stronger than cohesion, the liquid climbs the wall, forming a concave curve (meniscus) and generating a positive capillary pressure that pulls the fluid upward. The narrower the tube, the higher the liquid will rise. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·S. Michael Stewart +2
