## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

You are thinking of two fascinating but very different topics regarding isolation, hygiene, and immunity.

### 1. The "Dung Guy" Who Died After Bathing

The man you are referring to was named **Amou Haji**, an Iranian hermit who was widely dubbed by the media as the **"World's Dirtiest Man"**. 

![](../assets/b095ae2d9ff0b3ab6986.png)Facebook·Craft Factory

* **His Lifestyle:** Following emotional heartbreaks in his youth, Haji chose to live in extreme isolation in the desert village of Dezh Gah. He went **over 60 years without taking a bath**, firmly believing that soap and water would make him sick. He survived by eating roadkill (especially rotting porcupines), drinking water from rusty cans, and **smoking dried animal dung** out of an old pipe. 

  ![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +2
* **His Death:** In 2022, well-meaning local villagers finally pressured and persuaded him to take a bath for the first time in decades. Not long after, he fell ill and **passed away at the age of 94**. While the timing sparked a lot of urban legends that the bath killed him, doctors and researchers note there is no scientific proof of a direct medical link; his death was ultimately attributed to natural causes related to advanced old age. 

  ![](../assets/b095ae2d9ff0b3ab6986.png)Facebook +2

### 2. Uncontacted Tribes and Disease "Wipe Outs"

Your memory correctly connects the idea of a sudden change in environment/hygiene to the tragic history of **uncontacted Indigenous tribes**. However, for these tribes, the threat isn't soap and water—it is a lack of **immunological memory**. 

![](../assets/184f53f86ad13b8f1f32.png)Survival International

* **The Mechanism of Wipe Outs:** Because uncontacted groups live in complete isolation, their immune systems have never developed antibodies against common global illnesses. When loggers, miners, missionaries, or tourists make contact, they inadvertently introduce seemingly minor pathogens like **the common cold, influenza, or measles**. 

  ![](../assets/184f53f86ad13b8f1f32.png)Survival International +2
* **The Devastating Impact:** What is a mild cough to an outsider can act like a deadly plague to an isolated community. Historically and in modern times, first-contact scenarios have wiped out entire populations. According to reports by [Survival International](https://www.survivalinternational.org/), contact with outsiders has repeatedly wiped out upwards of **50% to 90% of a tribe's population** within just a few years due to rampant epidemics. 

  ![](../assets/5eb1ed0285a2aef1d19a.png)ictnews.org +3

While Amou Haji's body had decades to adapt to his own dirty micro-environment, uncontacted tribes face a much more fragile reality where external pathogens pose a literal threat of extinction. 

![](../assets/c686129a002a2c6610a4.png)PBS +1
