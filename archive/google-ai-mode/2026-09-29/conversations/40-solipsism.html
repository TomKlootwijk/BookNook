Response 1 · Google AI Mode
Solipsism is the philosophical position that only one's own mind is certain to exist. Derived from the Latin words sōlus ("alone") and ipse ("self"), it represents an extreme form of skepticism asserting that the external world and other minds cannot be truly known and might only be projections of one's own consciousness. 
Wikipedia +2

The Three Main Forms

Philosophers generally divide the concept into three primary variations: 
  • Metaphysical Solipsism: The most radical version. It claims that only your mind literally exists, and the entire universe—including other people—is a figment of your imagination or a mental representation. 
    Dictionary.com +1
  • Epistemological Solipsism: A more moderate stance. It concedes that an external reality might exist, but argues that it is entirely impossible to prove or truly know through our subjective senses. 
    Dictionary.com +1
  • Methodological Solipsism: A conceptual or scientific tool. It uses the self and internal mental processes as the only reliable starting point for constructing knowledge, popularized by René Descartes' famous declaration, "Cogito, ergo sum" ("I think, therefore I am"). 
    YouTube·Tech Guy Greg “TG2” +2

The Philosophical Dilemma: "The Problem of Other Minds"

Solipsism is famously difficult to logically disprove, earning it a reputation as an irrefutable but highly impractical theory. It highlights a fundamental asymmetry in human experience: you have direct, first-person access to your own thoughts and pain, but you can only infer the inner life of others through their outward behavior. 
John Horgan (The Science Writer) +1
To counter this, historical philosophers have proposed a few notable rebuttals: 
  • The Argument from Analogy: Bertrand Russell argued that because other people look like us and react to stimuli exactly as we do, it is reasonable to infer they possess minds similar to our own. 
  • The Common-Sense Approach: G.E. Moore argued that our immediate, physical interaction with the world and our own bodies provides an intuitive proof of reality that is more compelling than abstract skepticism. 
  • The Private Language Argument: Ludwig Wittgenstein suggested that the very existence of a shared language proves an external reality. Because language relies on public rules and shared meanings, a completely isolated, private mind could never invent a language to describe its own isolated experiences. 

Philosophy vs. Psychology

While solipsism is an intellectual thought experiment, it shares a boundary with psychology. Continual obsession with these thoughts can lead to a psychological state known as solipsism syndrome. Though not an officially classified psychiatric disorder, this syndrome causes individuals to feel completely detached and alienated from the outside world, mirroring elements of depersonalization-derealization disorder.
