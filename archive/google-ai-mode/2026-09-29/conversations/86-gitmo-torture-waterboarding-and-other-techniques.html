Response 1 · Google AI Mode
Following the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, the U.S. government, primarily through the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and Department of Defense (DoD), implemented a system of harsh interrogation methods. Officially termed "enhanced interrogation techniques" (EITs) by the Bush administration, these methods were utilized at secret global "black sites" and the Guantánamo Bay detention camp (Gitmo). 
A landmark 2014 U.S. Senate Intelligence Committee report and subsequent legal testimonies concluded that these techniques constituted torture, were reverse-engineered from military survival training designed to resist enemy interrogation, and were ineffective at gathering actionable intelligence.

Key Interrogation Techniques Used

TechniqueDescription & Application
WaterboardingSimulated drowning. A detainee is strapped to a reclined board with feet elevated. A cloth is placed over their nose and mouth while water is poured continuously to block airflow and trigger the primal panic of drowning. Notable high-value detainees like Khalid Shaikh Mohammed and Abu Zubaydah were waterboarded 183 times and 83 times, respectively.
Sleep DeprivationDetainees were kept awake for up to 11 consecutive days, often while shackled standing up, chained flat to a floor, or placed in stressful positions to break psychological resolve.
Cramped ConfinementPrisoners were forced into small, dark isolation boxes for hours or days. This included large coffin-sized boxes or tiny "dog boxes" that forced the body into an agonizing fetal position.
WallingInterrogators slung a flexible collar around the detainee's neck and used it to repeatedly slam their upper back and head against a false plywood wall to induce disorientation and fear.
Stress PositionsDetainees were forced into physically draining, painful body positions for extended periods, such as being handcuffed to high bars on tiptoes or being "short-shackled" to cell bars.
Forced Nudity & Dietary ManipulationStrip-searches and prolonged forced nakedness were used to systematically humiliate and psychologically weaken prisoners. Diapers were also used as a tool of humiliation. Detainees' caloric intake was tightly restricted.
Environmental ManipulationSubjection to extreme freezing temperatures, round-the-clock blaring loud music, or complete sensory deprivation in pitch-black rooms.
Unauthorized / Extralegal AbusesDocumentation from the U.S. Senate Report and detainee sketches revealed unauthorized physical violence, including "rectal hydration" (forced fluid administration), threats of sexual assault, power drill threats, and threats to desecrate the Qur'an.

Legal Context and Current Status

  • The "Torture Memos": In 2002, Justice Department lawyers John Yoo and Jay Bybee drafted legal memos arguing that physical pain only amounted to torture if it was equivalent to the intensity of organ failure or death. This legal framework was later withdrawn and heavily condemned. 
    Center for Constitutional Rights
  • Banning of the Program: On his second day in office in January 2009, President Barack Obama signed an Executive Order banning the use of enhanced interrogation techniques and closing the CIA's secret prisons. 
    BBC
  • Ongoing Judicial Impact: Legal battles continue at Guantánamo Bay. Military judges and defense lawyers frequently debate whether evidence and confessions obtained from defendants are legally inadmissible because they were tainted by the legacy of state-sponsored torture. 
    NPR +1
