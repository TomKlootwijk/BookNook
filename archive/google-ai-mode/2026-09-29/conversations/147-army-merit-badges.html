While the U.S. Army does not officially use the term "merit badges" for its active service members, the phrase typically connects to two distinct concepts: the official U.S. Army awards and qualification badges, or the Scouting America (BSA) Military Service merit badge. 

1. The U.S. Army Skill Badges & Historical "Badge of Military Merit"

Instead of merit badges, the U.S. Army utilizes a strict system of Combat and Special Skill Badges, identification badges, and marksmanship badges to recognize qualifications and accomplishments. 
Wikipedia
  • The Badge of Military Merit: Established by General George Washington in 1782, this was the first military decoration for common soldiers and ordinary infantry. It consisted of a purple, heart-shaped piece of silk and was eventually revived and redesigned by General Douglas MacArthur in 1932 as the modern Purple Heart Medal. 
  • The Legion of Merit: This is a highly distinguished military medal awarded for exceptionally meritorious conduct and outstanding service in chief positions of responsibility. 
    Army.mil +1
  • Modern Skill Badges: Soldiers earn specialized breast badges to show proficiency, such as the Combat Infantryman Badge, Airborne/Parachutist Badge, Air Assault Badge, or specialized attachments like the Army Astronaut Device. 
    Army.mil +1

2. Scouting America's "Military Service & Veterans" Merit Badge

If you are looking at youth scouting, Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America) introduced a specialized Military Service & Veterans Badge into its Scouts BSA Test Lab. 
Scouting America
  • Background: Launched following a collaborative agreement with the Department of Defense, this program invites Scouts to explore the history, sacrifice, and leadership values of the U.S. Armed Forces. 
    Scouting America +1
  • How it Works: Rather than using printed books, requirements are listed online via the Scouts BSA Test Lab. Scouts who complete the requirements earn a certificate of completion which retroactively converts to an official merit badge if the National Council permanently approves the topic. 
    Scouting America +1

While Scouting America does not have an exclusive "ADHD merit badge," neurodiversity, ADHD support, and adaptive advancement intersect directly with the Scouts BSA Test Lab and the broader merit badge pipeline in a few key ways. 

1. The "Life Skills" Test Lab Topic

The Scouts BSA Test Lab pilots potential new merit badges using digital resources rather than traditional printed pamphlets. One of the major pilot categories currently rotating through testing is Life Skills. This track focuses heavily on self-regulation, time management, organizational strategies, and executive functioning—skills that are incredibly relevant for Scouts navigating ADHD. 
Michigan Crossroads Council +1

2. Digital Transformation & Accessibility

The Test Lab is deliberately designed to be more accessible for neurodivergent youth. Instead of forcing Scouts to read long, text-heavy booklets, Test Lab requirements are hosted entirely online alongside supplementary videos and interactive digital content. This multimedia approach heavily benefits Scouts with ADHD who learn better through visual or auditory formats rather than traditional literature. 
YouTube·The Roaming Scouter +1

3. Flexible Test Lab Mentorship

For a standard merit badge, a Scout must find a specifically certified Merit Badge Counselor. However, under the Test Lab framework, any registered adult volunteer can guide the Scout and sign off on their requirements. This gives families the flexibility to pair a Scout with a trusted leader, parent, or mentor who already understands the Scout's specific ADHD triggers, focus windows, and accommodation needs. 
Scouting America +1

4. Official ADHD Accommodations (The Guide to Advancement)

If a Scout with ADHD is struggling with the specific wording or physical limitations of any standard or Test Lab merit badge, Scouting America has official safety valves built into its rules: 
YouTube·ScouterStan (Stan Richards)
  • No Time Extensions Needed: ADHD alone does not change the requirement to finish badges before turning 18, but the Test Lab framework allows self-paced tracking. 
    Scouting Newsroom +1
  • Alternative Requirements: According to Section 10 of the Guide to Advancement, if a documented disability (including severe ADHD) makes completing a specific merit badge requirement impossible, scouts can apply through their local council for an Individual Scout Advancement Plan (ISAP) to utilize alternative, equitable requirements. 
    YouTube·ScouterStan (Stan Richards)
