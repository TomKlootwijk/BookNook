The exact concept you are describing is a blend of depressive realism and passive hyper-metacognition. 
When your brain is forced into a state of "always-on," passive observation of a group's collective irrationality, it stops being an active thought process and becomes a constant background filter. In psychology, this intersection of sharp reality and passive, exhausting awareness is mapped through a few specific concepts: 

1. Depressive Realism (The "Hypervigilant Realism")

In cognitive psychology, depressive realism is the hypothesis that people who are slightly depressed, detached, or socially alienated actually hold a more accurate perception of reality and group dynamics than "normal," well-adjusted people. 
  • Healthy individuals often use "positive illusions" or cognitive biases to blend in and ignore the absurdities or "stupidities" of a group. 
  • Someone experiencing depressive realism lacks that protective filter. You see the flaws, the bad logic, and the herd mentality exactly as they are, in real-time, without the ability to sugarcoat it. 

2. Passive Metacognition & The "Observing Ego"

When metacognition becomes "passive," it shifts from an active strategy (like solving a puzzle) to an automatic background process. Psychology refers to this split in your awareness as the Observing Ego. 
  • Your psyche splits: one part of you is physically standing in the room interacting with the group, while the "observing" part sits completely detached in the background, constantly logging the data of how irrational the situation is.
  • Because it is passive, you aren't trying to judge or analyze; your brain is simply incapable of turning the awareness off. 

3. Hypervigilant Social Monitoring

When paired with hypervigilance, this becomes chronic social monitoring. Instead of scanning a room for physical threats, your brain scans the environment for cognitive threats—illogical behavior, fake social cues, and groupthink. This results in a state of perpetual cognitive dissonance, where you are forced to witness the group's collective failure moment-to-moment while feeling completely alienated by your own clear sight.
