When people ask for the "heaviest" chemical meaning the strongest or most powerful rather than the literal atomic weight, they are usually thinking of one of the following record-breakers in chemistry: 

1. The Strongest Acid: Fluoroantimonic Acid (HSbF6)

If you mean "strongest" as in the most corrosive, destructive acid, Fluoroantimonic acid is the undisputed champion among stable liquid superacids. 
Wikipedia +1
  • It is 2×1019 (20 quintillion) times stronger than 100% pure sulfuric acid.
  • It will instantly dissolve glass, skin, bone, and violently react with water. It can only be stored in specialized Teflon (PTFE) containers because the carbon-fluorine bonds in Teflon are too strong for it to break.
    (Note: Helium Hydride (HeH+) is theoretically the strongest molecule-sized acid in space, but it cannot be stored or used as a liquid chemical solution).     
    YouTube +5

2. The Strongest Chemical Bond: Carbon Monoxide (C≡O)

If you mean "strongest" in terms of how tightly two atoms are glued together, the triple bond between carbon and oxygen in carbon monoxide is the strongest chemical bond in nature. Breaking it requires a massive amount of energy (1072 kJ/mol). In organic chemistry specifically, the Carbon-Fluorine (C−F) bond is the strongest single bond. 
Khan Academy

3. The Strongest Explosive: Heptanitrocubane / Azidoazide Azide

If you mean "strongest" as in the chemical that packs the most destructive explosive power: 
  • Heptanitrocubane is considered the most powerful non-nuclear explosive material, though it is incredibly rare and expensive.
  • Azidoazide Azide (C2N14) is famously known as the most reactive/unstable explosive chemical ever created; it will detonate from the slightest touch, light exposure, or even just sitting undisturbed in a lab. 
    Quora

4. The Strongest Material: Carbyne / Graphene

If you mean "strongest" as in structural tensile strength, Carbyne (a chain of carbon atoms linked by alternating triple and single bonds) is theoretically the strongest material in existence, followed closely by Graphene, which is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice that is 200 times stronger than steel.
