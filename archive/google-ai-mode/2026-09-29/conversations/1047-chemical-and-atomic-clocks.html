Chemical clocks and atomic clocks do not create inanimate objects or biological life. Instead, they are highly precise tools used by scientists to measure time across vastly different scales. 
The phrase "tau tau mu mu" (ττμμ) refers to fundamental subatomic particles called leptons (specifically, pairs of tau leptons and muons) studied in quantum physics, but they do not act as templates for creating life or matter.

The Physics Context: ττμμ

In high-energy physics experiments (like those at the Large Hadron Collider), tracking the decay of heavy particles into tau leptons (τ) and muons (μ) helps physicists understand the fundamental forces of the universe. While these particles are the building blocks of the universe's matter fields, they exist at a subatomic layer far below the complex chemistry required to form either inanimate objects or biological organisms. 
Biological life relies heavily on biochemical rhythms (like circadian rhythms), which operate more like complex, organic "chemical clocks," while atomic clocks remain purely technological instruments used for physics and measurement.
