**Power Grid** is a highly acclaimed, strategic economic board game designed by **Friedemann Friese** and published by [Rio Grande Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_Games). Originally released in German as *Funkenschlag*, the game challenges 2 to 6 players to build a massive electrical network, buy fuel, and upgrade power plants to supply the most cities with electricity.

### Core Gameplay Mechanics

The game unfolds across three distinct phases each round, demanding careful budgeting and ruthless tactical planning:

* **The Auction:** Players bid against each other to buy increasingly efficient power plants from a dynamic real-time market. Keeping an eye on your opponent's cash flow is essential here.
* **Resource Management:** Players buy raw materials like **coal, oil, garbage, and uranium** to fuel their plants. The twist? It's a supply-and-demand economy—the more resource tokens players buy, the more expensive those resources become for everyone else.
* **Network Building:** Players pay money to connect physical cities on a map grid. The game engine features a brilliant rubber-band mechanic: the player in last place gets to buy resources and expand their network first, while the leader is forced to pay premium prices.

### Versions and Extensions

* **Power Grid: Recharged Edition:** The modern retail baseline version. It updates the rulebook to streamline small mechanical quirks while preserving the classic economic gameplay loop.
* **Map Expansions:** One of the game's greatest features is its modular nature. While the base game includes a double-sided map of the **USA and Germany**, there are dozens of expansion boards available (including Europe, China, Brazil, and the Middle East), each introducing unique geographic hurdles and local resource market rules.
* **Power Grid: The Card Game:** A streamlined, faster-paced alternative that entirely removes the map-building element, focusing heavily on plant auctions and resource management.

In **Power Grid**, the mechanical components translate into a very specific set of **player affordances**:

### 📈 Economic Maneuvering & Leverage

* **Hording / Cornering the Market:** Because resources have a strict supply-and-demand curve, you can deliberately buy up all of a cheap resource (like oil or coal) even if you don't fully need it. Your affordance here is to artificially starve your opponents, driving up their operational costs.
* **Hate-Bidding (Spite Auctions):** The auction system allows you to intentionally bid up the price of a power plant you have no intention of keeping, forcing a wealthy opponent to overpay and deplete their cash reserves before the building phase.

### 🗺️ Spatial Aggression & Blocking

* **Choke-Pointing:** The map network isn't just a passive puzzle; it's a shared territory. The game affords you the ability to identify bottleneck connections between regions and buy into them early, effectively boxing an opponent into a corner of the map or forcing them to pay massive connection premiums to bypass you.
* **Encircle and Starve:** You can deliberately expand your network around an opponent's hub to completely halt their growth, capitalizing on the rule that cities can only hold a limited number of players depending on the current "Step" of the game.

### 🕒 Temporal Control & Turn-Order Manipulation

* **Throwing the Race (The Rubber-Band Dance):** Because the game brutally punishes the leader (making them bid last, buy resources last at the highest prices, and build last), the game affords you the unique strategy of *intentional underperformance*. You can choose to deliberately stall your expansion, staying one city behind the leader, just to guarantee you get the cheapest resources and the best power plants on the next turn.
* **Triggering Phase Changes:** You have the agency to decide exactly *when* the game progresses. By building the 7th city (triggering Step 2) or buying a specific plant, you can force the game state to shift when your opponents are financially unready.

### 💰 Risk Management & Calculus

* **The "Zero-Cash" Gamble:** Power Grid allows for precise mathematical certainty. You can calculate exactly how much money you will earn at the end of a round. This affords you the ability to spend down to your absolute last Electro ($0), knowing your network will reliably catch you—or risking a complete blowout if someone outbids you by $1.
