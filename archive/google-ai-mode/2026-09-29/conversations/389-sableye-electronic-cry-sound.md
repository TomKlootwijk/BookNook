Bismuth naturally crystallizes in a **trigonal/rhombohedral** system, not a true cubic one. However, it looks like a series of concentric, hollowed-out stair-step cubes because of **hopper growth**:

1. **High Surface Energy at Edges:** When molten bismuth cools, the atoms find it energetically easier to attach themselves to the outer edges and corners of the developing crystal structure rather than filling in the flat faces.
2. **The Spiral Growth Mechanism:** The crystallization propagates outward via **screw dislocations**. This layer-by-layer spiral growth can be mathematically mapped using **polar coordinates**  $(r, \theta)$  where the radius expands logarithmically as a function of the angle (θ).
3. **The Resulting Hollow Structure:** Because the perimeter grows exponentially faster than the center, it leaves a hollow, terraced depression on each face, creating the illusion of interlocking geometric puzzle cubes.

### The "Logarithmic Spiral" of Growth

The step-like concentric squares of a bismuth crystal closely mimic a **discrete logarithmic spiral** projected onto a 3D orthogonal grid. The growth rate outward from the core follows a spiral pattern, but the atomic bonding forces force those spirals into rigid, 90-degree stair-steps.

### The Story Behind the Rainbow Colors

The mesmerizing metallic colors are entirely separate from the geometric encoding. They are caused by **thin-film interference**:

* As the hot bismuth makes contact with the air, a microscopic layer of **bismuth oxide** forms on the surface.

* When light reflects off both the top of the oxide layer and the underlying bismuth metal, the light waves interfere with each other, canceling out certain wavelengths and reflecting brilliant neons (blues, purples, golds, and greens) depending on exactly how thick the oxide layer is at that specific spot.
