Response 1 · Google AI Mode
The condition you are describing is commonly known on the streets as the "fentanyl fold" or "fenty lean". It is a physical state where an individual under the influence of synthetic opioids bends rigidly forward at the waist, remaining frozen or slumped in an extreme, unnatural posture for long periods. 
Addiction Center +1
While the most visible effect is the severe forward hunch, the structural impact on the body and the abdomen involves several interconnected medical factors: 
Healthy Life Recovery
  • Muscle Rigidity and Dystonia: Synthetic opioids like fentanyl frequently trigger severe, involuntary muscle contractions and rigidity, specifically targeting the trunk, chest, and abdominal muscles. This is sometimes colloquially or clinically related to "wooden chest syndrome" (where chest and abdominal walls freeze tight) or opioid-induced hypertonia. 
    National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +3
  • Core Muscle Weakness: Paradoxically, as the drug induces deep central nervous system depression, the core muscles responsible for keeping a person upright lose tone entirely. The back and abdominal muscles can no longer support the skeletal structure. 
    www.addictionresource.net +1
  • Intra-Abdominal Pressure: When a person is severely bent over at the waist for hours at a time, the physical folding forces the internal organs downward and outward against the abdominal wall. Combined with increased internal abdominal pressure caused by opioid-induced muscle spasms and chronic constipation/intestinal obstruction (ileus), the abdomen can appear distended, bloated, or as though it is pushing out forcefully. 
    National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +3
  • Exacerbation of Hernias: If an individual already has an existing lower back injury or an abdominal wall hernia, this extreme posture and intense muscle strain significantly worsen the protrusion, making the internal tissue bulge out dramatically. 
    OHSU +1

Prompt 2 · You
yes but literally when the lower back and front muscles just give up and abdomens bulge out
Response 2 · Google AI Mode
When someone experiencing chronic synthetic opioid use reaches the point where their core muscles literally "give up," it creates a stark physical transformation. This occurs through a combination of profound neurological depression, muscle failure, and internal pressure. 
When the lower back and front muscles lose all tone while the abdomen bulges outward, the following medical changes are occurring simultaneously: 

1. Neurological "Flaccid Paralysis" of Postural Muscles

Fentanyl heavily depresses the central nervous system. For a chronic user, this causes a complete drop in motor control and muscle tone. The erector spinae (the long muscles running along the spine that keep humans upright) and the rectus abdominis (the "six-pack" front muscles) essentially go limp. Because the nervous system stops sending signals to keep these muscles tense, the core undergoes an involuntary structural collapse. 
www.addictionresource.net +2

2. Muscle Atrophy and Malnutrition

Long-term fentanyl use frequently causes opioid-induced endocrinopathy. This condition severely lowers sex hormones (like testosterone), which—combined with chronic malnutrition and deep sedation—leads to rapid muscle wasting (atrophy). The muscular wall of the abdomen physically thins out and weakens, losing the structural integrity required to hold internal organs tightly inside the abdominal cavity. 
Legacy Recovery Center

3. Severe Opioid-Induced Bowel Dysfunction (OIBD)

As the external structural muscles fail, massive pressure builds from the inside out. Opioids paralyze the gastrointestinal tract, causing severe, chronic opioid-induced constipation and ileus (a complete lack of movement in the intestines). This leads to: 
National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +2
  • Extreme abdominal distension due to trapped solid waste and massive gas accumulation.
  • The completely relaxed, thinned-out abdominal wall yielding entirely to this internal pressure, causing the stomach area to physically bloat and bulge outward. 
    You and Constipation·You and Constipation

4. Gravitational and Positional Force

Because the back muscles have collapsed, the individual is frequently stuck in the forward-bent "fentanyl fold" position for hours. In this sharply bent posture, gravity and the literal bending of the spine push the heavy, distended internal organs downward and forward. Without functional abdominal muscles to act as a girdle, the organs push directly against the weak skin and fascia of the lower belly, creating a dramatic, protruding appearance. If a hernia is already present in the lower back or groin, this combination of gravity, internal pressure, and muscle failure forces the tissue out extensively.
