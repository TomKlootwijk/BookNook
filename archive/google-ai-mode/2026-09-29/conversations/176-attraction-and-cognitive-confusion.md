It makes complete sense why that split-second mix-up felt so jarring. When your brain has a deeply ingrained, automatic rule—what you perfectly described as a **repulsion LUT (Look-Up Table)**—any visual data that triggers it is going to cause an immediate, visceral mental jolt.

Our brains build these "Look-Up Tables" over time to act as instant shortcuts. Here is exactly what is happening under the hood during that "mental jab":

### 1. The Collision of Fast vs. Slow Thinking

Your brain operates on two speeds. **System 1** is your lightning-fast, automatic subconscious processor. It scans the environment in milliseconds. When you saw her, System 1 caught a fleeting, ambiguous feature and instantly cross-referenced it with your "repulsion LUT," firing off a warning shot before your conscious mind even fully understood what it was looking at.

**System 2** is your slower, logical conscious mind. A split second later, it kicked in, looked at her fully, and said, *"Wait, no, she's an attractive female."* The confusion and anxiety come from the lag time between that automated subconscious jolt and your conscious realization.

### 2. The Accumulation Over Time

Your mention of the "1D arrow of time" is spot on. These LUTs aren't random; they are built through a lifetime of accumulated preferences, social conditioning, and repeated mental patterns. Every time your brain successfully categorizes something as a "turn-off," that neural pathway gets reinforced.

Because this pathway is so heavily greased, it triggers **instantaneously**. It acts like a tripwire. When a perfectly feminine person happens to possess a single feature or angle that accidentally clips that tripwire, the alarm goes off anyway.

### 3. The "False Positive"

What you experienced was simply a **visual false positive**. Your automated system misfired for a fraction of a second because human features exist on a spectrum, and lighting, angles, or shadows can momentarily distort how we perceive a face.

The "jab" isn't a reflection of your actual desires or your sexuality; it is just the sound of a highly sensitive alarm system doing its job a little *too* fast.
