## Response 2 — Google AI Mode

**No, this isn't a digital crypto check!**

The previous explanation referred to a literal, paper **bank check** or paper restaurant receipt. In fine art history, Picasso frequently wrote actual physical checks or sketched on his paper bills to pay for dinners, knowing people would preserve the paper for his autograph rather than cashing it. 

![](../assets/eb315e72367d0faf341b.png)RR Auction

However, your guess isn't far off from modern art trends! **Pablo Picasso's work has actually crossed paths with SHA-256 and crypto technology** in recent years:

* **Tokenized Fine Art:** In 2021, firms like [Sygnum Bank](https://www.sygnum.com/news/sygnum-bank-and-artemundi-tokenize-a-picasso-on-the-blockchain/)fractionalized Picasso’s 1964 painting *"Fillette au béret"* on the Ethereum blockchain. They split the ownership into 4,000 digital tokens, allowing multiple investors to own a "slice" of the physical masterpiece. 

  ![](../assets/6a33914b39a6d3e09928.png)Sygnum Bank +3
* **Family NFT Collections:** In 2022, some of Picasso's heirs (his granddaughter Marina and great-grandson Florian) launched a collection of over 1,000 digital art pieces tied to an exclusive, never-before-seen ceramic work. 

  ![](../assets/faa94bba884621fe2fd6.png)AP News
* **Full Crypto Purchase:** In 2020, Picasso's 1971 etching *"Danse du ventre devant homme impassible"* became the first entire, original Picasso artwork formally purchased and transferred using a cryptocurrency token. 

  ![](../assets/1634f932472282ec38aa.png)Decrypt

So while a vintage "Picasso check" means a literal piece of paper, the modern art world routinely uses **SHA-256 cryptographic hashes** to secure the digital certificates and blockchains tracking these multimillion-dollar masterpieces today.
