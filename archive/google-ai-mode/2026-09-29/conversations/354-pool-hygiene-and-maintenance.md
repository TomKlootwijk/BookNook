## Prompt 1 — You

when someone has her period in the pool or shits in the pool what happens to the pool

## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

**The short answer is that a little bit of period blood is easily neutralized by standard pool chemicals, but poop triggers a strict health-and-safety shutdown.**

Because these two situations are handled completely differently, here is exactly what happens to the pool in each scenario:

### 🩸 Scenario 1: Someone has their period in the pool

If a small amount of menstrual blood leaks into the pool, **essentially nothing happens to the water**.

* **The Myth of "Dying" the Water:** Despite popular myths, getting your period in a pool [will not turn the water red or trigger a colored dye cloud](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskReddit/comments/m1917h/when_girls_get_periods_in_the_pool_does_it_dye/).
* **The Water Pressure Factor:** While a person is submerged, the natural hydrostatic pressure of the water temporarily slows or holds back the menstrual flow. 

  ![](../assets/0fed72099beca178ed8c.png)Healthline +1
* **Chemical Cleanup:** If any blood does escape, it is instantly diluted by thousands of gallons of water. Furthermore, the pool’s built-in chlorine or bromine sanitizer immediately kills any potential bacteria or pathogens, making it completely safe for other swimmers.

### 💩 Scenario 2: Someone poops in the pool

If someone poops in the pool, **the pool must be closed immediately.** Feces can carry dangerous germs like *Cryptosporidium*, *Giardia*, and *E. coli*, which cause severe diarrheal illnesses. 

![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)

When a "fecal incident" happens, lifeguards and pool operators must strictly follow public health procedures, such as the [CDC's Healthy Swimming Guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/response/what-to-do-when-there-is-poop-in-the-pool.html):

1. **Evacuation:** The pool is immediately cleared of all swimmers. 

   ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)
2. **Physical Removal:** Staff wear gloves and use a scoop net or bucket to manually remove the poop. They will **never use a pool vacuum**, as that would spread the contamination throughout the internal plumbing system. 

   ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)
3. **Chemical Hyper-Chlorination:**
   * **For Solid Poop:** The pool operator will raise the free chlorine levels to **2 parts per million (ppm)** and ensure the pH is at 7.5 or less. The water must then circulate for **at least 30 minutes** before anyone can swim again.
   * **For Liquid Poop (Diarrhea):** This is a much higher biohazard risk. The pool must be closed for **up to 13 hours** or more while chlorine levels are jacked up exceptionally high to kill off stubborn germs like *Cryptosporidium*. 

     ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)
4. **Filtration:** The pool's filtration system is kept running continuously to scrub the water clean before reopening. 

   ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)
