### 🧠 The Overlap: Where "Chicken" Meets "Russian Roulette"

When people describe a situation as "a game of chicken like Russian roulette," they are usually referring to **Brinkmanship**—a scenario where human decisions intentionally introduce a dangerous element of random chance.

However, to win a game of Chicken, a player might intentionally make the outcome random to terrify the opponent. For example, a driver in a game of Chicken might visibly **rip the steering wheel off** and throw it out the window. By doing this, they turn a game of psychological choice (Chicken) into an unstoppable gamble of pure chance (Russian roulette), forcing the other driver to swerve.

It is a game of Chicken because your speed relies on nerve and willpower, but it acts like Russian Roulette because a single slip-up results in immediate, self-inflicted physical injury.

In these games, a multiplier climbs higher and higher. The player must decide when to cash out (Chicken) before the system randomly crashes and wipes out everything (Russian Roulette).
