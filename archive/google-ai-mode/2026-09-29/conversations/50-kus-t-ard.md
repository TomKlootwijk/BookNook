**Custard** is a smooth, creamy dessert or sauce made primarily from a cooked mixture of **milk, cream, sugar, and egg yolks**. 

![](../assets/4af75d07c5019da1cf6b.png)RecipeTin Eats +1

The thickness of a traditional custard comes from the coagulation of egg proteins as they are heated gently. Depending on how much egg or starch is used, it can range from a thin, pourable dessert sauce (**Crème Anglaise**) to a thick pastry filling (**Crème Pâtissière**). 

![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +3

### Simple Stovetop Vanilla Custard Recipe

If you want to make an easy, classic pouring custard at home, here is what you will need: 

![](../assets/b2572e24f05666f42c0e.png)Good Food

#### 🛒 Ingredients

* **2 cups (500ml)** whole milk or a mix of milk and heavy cream
* **3 to 4** large egg yolks
* **¼ cup (50g)** sugar
* **1 to 2 teaspoons** cornstarch (optional, helps prevent curdling and adds thickness)
* **1 teaspoon** vanilla extract or paste 

  ![](../assets/7c9b8d4d7e264c254c62.png)Sweetest Menu +3

#### 🍳 Quick Steps

1. **Heat the dairy:** Warm your milk (and cream, if using) in a saucepan over medium-low heat until it is steaming, but do not let it boil. 

   ![](../assets/c5a2c56da6f594968c03.png)Taming Twins
2. **Whisk the base:** In a separate bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, sugar, cornstarch, and vanilla until smooth and slightly pale. 

   ![](../assets/c5a2c56da6f594968c03.png)Taming Twins
3. **Temper the eggs:** Slowly pour a steady splash of the warm milk into your egg mixture while whisking constantly. This warms the eggs gently so they don't scramble. 

   ![](../assets/c5a2c56da6f594968c03.png)Taming Twins
4. **Thicken:** Pour the entire mixture back into the saucepan. Cook over low heat, stirring continuously with a wooden spoon or whisk until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon. Do not let it boil. 

   ![](../assets/4af75d07c5019da1cf6b.png)RecipeTin Eats +1
5. **Serve:** Enjoy it hot over pies and crumbles, or chill it in the fridge with a layer of plastic wrap touching the surface to prevent a skin from forming. 

   ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Downshiftology

**"Tardnation strength"** (often discussed online as a variation of "tard strength") is an internet slang term used to describe the perception of **exceptional or surprising physical strength** in individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities, such as Down syndrome.

From a medical and scientific perspective, this phenomenon is not caused by having larger muscles or unique physical structures. Instead, it is typically explained by a combination of the following factors:

* **Overriding Biological Inhibitors:** In everyday life, the human brain puts natural "brakes" on our muscles to prevent us from ripping our own tendons or damaging muscle tissue. During high-stress moments—or when neurological differences impair the brain's internal feedback mechanism ("this hurts, stop")—a person can access a much higher percentage of their absolute muscular capability. This is similar to the "hysterical strength" seen when a parent lifts a car off a child in an emergency.
* **Leverage and Body Composition:** Certain conditions can alter bone density or body proportions, which sometimes gives an advantage in low-leverage physical tasks (like pushing or holding something down).
* **Lack of Social Restraint:** A neurotypical adult might hold back their full physical power during an altercation or task out of social awareness or fear of consequences. Someone who does not process those specific social boundaries may exert 100% of their physical power immediately.
