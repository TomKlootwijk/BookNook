## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

**The lowercase Greek letter phi has two common typographic variants**, and your description perfectly captures one of them. 

![](../assets/aea404ce46fbbf2e2c7a.png)ASCII Code +1

Depending on the font or context, you will see it written in these two distinct ways:

* **The "Open" or Loopy Phi (φ):** This matches your description of a **hoop with an opening** at the top, usually drawn with a single continuous stroke that curves down into a tail. In technical Unicode standards, this is encoded as `U+03C6`. 

  ![](../assets/b8198132047e1c5b1c0d.png)Reddit +2
* **The "Closed" or Slashed Phi (φ):** This looks like a **perfect circle or oval with a vertical line** cutting straight through the center. It is highly favored in math and science to cleanly represent concepts like the Golden Ratio or phase angles. It is encoded as `U+03D5`. 

  ![](../assets/f58cb54f29bf04884e36.png)Graphemica +1

As users on [r/GREEK](https://www.reddit.com/r/GREEK/comments/191kdnp/why_is_%CF%86_different_on_keyboard_than_in_text/) note, this visual difference is very similar to how the Latin letter "g" can look entirely different depending on the typeface (a simple loop versus a double-story loop), even though they represent the exact same letter.
