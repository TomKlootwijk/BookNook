While **upside-down pineapple magnets** are widely placed on cruise ship doors by passengers to signal they are swingers, a **WiFi Pineapple** is something entirely different—and significantly more dangerous. 

![](../assets/b095ae2d9ff0b3ab6986.png)Facebook +2

The two terms are completely unrelated, though both can involve the cruise corridor:

### 1. The Cruise Door Pineapple Magnet (The Lifestyle Signal)

* **What it is:** A simple, decorative refrigerator-style magnet.
* **The Purpose:** Passengers buy [pineapple cruise door magnets](https://www.amazon.com/upside-down-pineapple-magnets-cruise-door/s?k=upside+down+pineapple+magnets+for+cruise+door) to stick on their metallic cabin doors. When flipped upside down, it is used strictly for consensual adult matchmaking. 

  ![](../assets/7e67fb8e6c43002e09c7.png)Amazon.com +5

### 2. The WiFi Pineapple (The Cybersecurity Threat)

* **What it is:** A specialized, pocket-sized wireless hacking tool developed by cybersecurity firm [Hak5](https://shop.hak5.org/products/pager). It has absolutely nothing to do with swinging. 

  ![](../assets/1eae20ad3b5d667bd411.jpg)Securiguide +1
* **How it works:** It mimics legitimate public Wi-Fi networks (like a cruise ship's guest internet). When your phone or laptop tries to connect to the ship's Wi-Fi, the WiFi Pineapple tricks your device into connecting to *it* instead. 

  ![](../assets/1eae20ad3b5d667bd411.jpg)Securiguide
* **The Danger:** Once a passenger's device connects to a rogue WiFi Pineapple, the person operating it can execute "Man-in-the-Middle" attacks. They can intercept your internet traffic, harvest passwords, and capture personal data while you think you are just browsing the web. 

  ![](../assets/1eae20ad3b5d667bd411.jpg)Securiguide +1

### The Cruise Connection

Because cruise ship stateroom walls and doors are made of metal, regular magnets stick to them perfectly. 

![](../assets/b095ae2d9ff0b3ab6986.png)Facebook

However, a **WiFi Pineapple** is electronic hardware—not a flat door magnet. If a malicious actor were to hide a WiFi Pineapple device in a corridor or a public lounge, they could easily compromise the personal data of nearby travelers who are casually trying to connect to the ship's network. To stay safe from rogue devices on a cruise, it is always highly recommended to use a Virtual Private Network (VPN) when logging onto ship Wi-Fi. 

![](../assets/1eae20ad3b5d667bd411.jpg)Securiguide +2
