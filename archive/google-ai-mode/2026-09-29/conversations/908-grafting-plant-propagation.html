In plant propagation, transplanting and grafting serve entirely different purposes but are frequently combined to maximize crop yields, disease resistance, and plant health. 
tind.io +1

The Core Difference

  • Grafting is the surgical process of joining two separate living plants together. The upper portion (the scion) provides the desired fruits or leaves, while the lower portion (the rootstock) provides a strong root system. They must heal together so their vascular cambium layers align and fuse. 
    Mississippi State Extension Service +2
  • Transplanting is the physical act of moving a plant from one location (like a small nursery pot or a healing chamber) into its permanent home in a larger container or open soil. 
    wikiHow +1

How They Work Together

When managing grafted plants, you must follow a highly precise timeline before, during, and after transplanting: 
PhaseCore ObjectiveKey Instructions
1. The GraftStructural FusionCut the scion and rootstock at matching angles. Secure them tightly using Grafting Clips or Tape to keep the cambium layers pressed together.
2. HealingVascular ReconnectionPlace the plant into a high-humidity, shaded healing chamber for 4 to 7 days. This stops the scion from drying out while the two independent vascular networks fuse into one.
3. TransplantingField EstablishmentOnce the graft union has fully healed, move the seedling to its final destination. Crucial Rule: Always ensure the graft union stays above the soil line to prevent the scion from sending out its own roots and bypassing the beneficial rootstock.
