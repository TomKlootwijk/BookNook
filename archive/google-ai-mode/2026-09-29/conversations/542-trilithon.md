## Response 2 — Google AI Mode

Yes, **hunebedden are very similar** to trilithons!

Both architectural structures use the classic **post-and-lintel system**—vertical upright stones supporting horizontal capstones. However, they belong to different architectural styles and served different primary purposes:

### 🏛️ Key Differences

* **Structure & Design:** A trilithon is strictly a **freestanding three-stone unit** (two uprights, one lintel), often built out of carefully shaped, squared-off stones. A hunebed is a **prehistoric chamber** built from raw, unshaped glacial boulders. It uses many upright stones and multiple capstones to form a long, enclosed room.
* **Function:** Trilithons (like those at Stonehenge) often served as monument markers, gateways, or astronomical alignments. Hunebedden were built primarily as **collective passage graves** (tombs) where communities buried their dead over generations.
* **Original Appearance:** While trilithons stood completely exposed, hunebedden were originally **buried under mounds of earth and turf** (tumuli), leaving only the entrance visible. Over thousands of years, the soil eroded away, leaving the skeleton of stones you see in the Netherlands today.
