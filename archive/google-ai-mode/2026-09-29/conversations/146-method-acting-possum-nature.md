While we often call them nature’s finest **method actors**, when an opossum "plays dead," it isn't actually acting at all—**it is a completely involuntary, automatic physiological defense mechanism** triggered by extreme fear and stress. 

![](../assets/b095ae2d9ff0b3ab6986.png)Facebook·For Fox Sake Wildlife Rescue +1

Scientifically known as **thanatosis** or **tonic immobility**, this response is the biological equivalent of fainting or going into shock. The opossum’s nervous system takes over entirely without its "permission," meaning the animal cannot simply choose to snap out of it or control how long it lasts. 

![](../assets/7af469f87837a11ceb56.png)www.grunge.com +4

### Inside the "Performance"

When an opossum enters this catatonic state, its body pulls out all the stops to convince predators it is a rotting, unappetizing corpse: 

![](../assets/b64f829a692513b4962f.png)Instagram +1

* **The Physical Stupor:** They keel over onto their side, their muscles go stiff or completely limp, their eyes freeze open, and their tongue turns blue and dangles out of an open mouth. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Crazy Creatures +2
* **Vitals Drop:** Their breathing and heart rate slow down so dramatically that they become barely perceptible. 

  ![](../assets/7b91c646e3d35cb57506.png)AskNature.org
* **Pain Insensitivity:** They temporarily stop blinking and do not feel pain or respond to touch, leaving them completely defenseless if a predator continues to bite or nudge them. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Know The Nature +2
* **The Smell Test:** To seal the deal, their bowels empty and they secrete a foul, green fluid from their anal glands that smells exactly like decaying meat. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Crazy Creatures +2

### Why the Strategy Works

Most predators are biologically hardwired to either chase moving prey or avoid eating already dead, rotting flesh that could harbor harmful toxins. When a coyote, fox, or dog encounters a stiff, foul-smelling opossum, they usually lose interest and move on. 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Animal Doozy +2

The state can last anywhere from **a few minutes to four hours**. Throughout the entire ordeal, the opossum actually remains semi-conscious and alert enough to monitor its surroundings. Once its nervous system senses that the danger has safely passed, the animal "comes back to life" and quickly scrambles to safety. Interestingly, this extreme response is most common in younger opossums; older individuals are much more likely to stand their ground, hiss, and bare their 50 sharp teeth.
