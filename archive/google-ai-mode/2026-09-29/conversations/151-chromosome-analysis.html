Response 1 · Google AI Mode
A chromosome is a threadlike structure of nucleic acids and protein found in the nucleus of most living cells, carrying genetic information in the form of genes. Without this packaging, DNA molecules would be too long to fit inside the cell. For example, if all of the DNA molecules in a single human cell were unwound, they would stretch 6 feet long. 
National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) (.gov) +1

🧬 Structure of a Chromosome

Chromosomes possess a unique, highly organized structure that prevents long strands of DNA from tangling: 
YouTube·HealthTree Foundation for Multiple Myeloma +1
  • DNA and Histones: The DNA molecule is tightly wrapped around spool-like proteins called histones, forming a combination called chromatin.
  • Centromere: A constricted region that divides the chromosome into two arms and serves as an attachment point during cell division.
  • P arm & Q arm: The shorter arm is designated as the p arm (petit), and the longer arm is the q arm.
  • Telomeres: Repetitive DNA sequences at the caps of the chromosome arms that protect the genetic material from degradation. 
    Cleveland Clinic +3

👥 Human Chromosomes

Humans typically have 46 chromosomes in almost every cell, organized into 23 pairs. You inherit one chromosome per pair from each biological parent: 
MedlinePlus (.gov) +2
  • Autosomes (Pairs 1–22): These 44 chromosomes contain genes that dictate general physical traits and metabolic functions (e.g., eye color, height). 
    YouTube·Amoeba Sisters +1
  • Sex Chromosomes (Pair 23): These determine biological sex. Biologically female individuals usually have two X chromosomes (XX), while biologically male individuals usually have one X and one Y chromosome (XY). 
    National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) (.gov) +1

🧫 Functional Classification

Depending on where the centromere is positioned along the length of the chromosome, it can be categorized into four primary types:
