Response 3 · Google AI Mode
From a strictly scientific and philosophical standpoint, no, a psychological feeling cannot break the chain of physical determinism. 
If hard determinism is true, your internal feeling that "35 means -1" is not a freewill rebellion against the laws of physics. Instead, that very feeling is the inevitable byproduct of a complex chain reaction: your brain's unique neural wiring, past memories, chemical state, and genetic makeup. In this view, your brain was always physically bound to generate that exact thought at this exact moment. 
However, if you want to find some philosophical "wiggling room," thinkers generally point to three ideas: 

1. Compatibilism (Changing the Definition of Free Will)

Most modern philosophers are compatibilists. They argue that determinism can coexist with free will. Even if your feeling was physically predetermined by your brain chemistry, it is still your feeling. If you act on your unique psychological map without outside force or coercion, you are exercising free will in a meaningful way, even if the universe's physics laws are set in stone. 

2. Quantum Indeterminism (The Microscopic Wildcard)

At the foundation of reality, quantum mechanics shows that subatomic particles operate on probabilities, not strict certainty. While many scientists argue that these microscopic random behaviors cancel out by the time they reach large objects like brain cells, some physicists and philosophers suggest that the brain might leverage quantum randomness. If true, the universe isn't strictly deterministic, though random particle behavior doesn't automatically equal conscious free will. 

3. Subjective Reality vs. Objective Physics

While physics rules the external universe, psychology rules your internal experience. If your brain decides that 35 means -1, that association becomes a functional reality for how you navigate your thoughts, creativity, and emotions. Determinism might dictate the machinery of your brain, but your subjective consciousness is the arena where you actually live.
