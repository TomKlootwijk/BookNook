## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

**Kamikaze pilots were prepared through intense psychological conditioning, rigid societal pressure, and brutal military training rather than being routinely "wired" on drugs**. While there is a common myth that they were heavily drugged or drunk during their final flights, historical evidence shows their compliance was engineered through deep cultural and systemic indoctrination. 

![](../assets/e138d1ff5fc11dc46a25.png)International Journal of Naval History +3

### 💊 The Reality of Drug Use

The idea that Kamikaze pilots were pumped full of drugs to force them into compliance is largely inaccurate. 

![](../assets/f6a804554b040fab89c6.png)HistoryNet +1

* **Methamphetamines (*Hiropon*):** The Japanese military did legally manufacture and use methamphetamines during World War II to combat fatigue and keep night fighter pilots alert. However, it was not routinely used to force or steel suicide attackers for their final missions. 

  ![](../assets/f6a804554b040fab89c6.png)HistoryNet
* **The Ritual Sake:** Historical accounts and propaganda often highlight the farewell ceremony where pilots drank **sake** (rice wine). This was a ceremonial toast of finality, not an attempt to get the pilots drunk. 

  ![](../assets/5858380bb8e326073a64.png)Navy History (.mil) +3
* **Debunking the "Automatons" Myth:** Historians emphasize that these men were not chained into their cockpits or flying in drug-induced deliriams; they were fully aware of their actions, operating out of a complex web of duty, fear, and patriotism. 

  ![](../assets/7861cff9e1816e7201f0.png)Quora +2

### 🧠 Cultural and State Indoctrination

The Imperial Japanese military leveraged powerful cultural frameworks to make self-sacrifice seem like the ultimate honorable choice: 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Australian Military Aviation History

* **The Bushido Code:** The military heavily idealized **Bushido** (the samurai way of the warrior), reshaping a historical warrior ethos into a national code of conduct. Death for the Emperor was framed as the ultimate purification and a noble duty. 

  ![](../assets/e138d1ff5fc11dc46a25.png)International Journal of Naval History +1
* **State Shintoism:** Pilots were taught that dying for the Emperor—who was viewed as a living deity—would elevate their souls to become *eirei* (guardian war gods) at the Yasukuni Shrine. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·FRANCE 24 English +1
* **Reversing the Narrative of Death:** Trainers actively desensitized pilots to mortality. They recast death not as an end, but as a transition to eternal honor and a necessary shield to protect their families from invading Allied forces. 

  ![](../assets/7861cff9e1816e7201f0.png)Quora +1

### 👥 Systemic Coercion and Peer Pressure

While the state portrayed all Kamikazes as eager volunteers, the reality was deeply coercive. 

![](../assets/b8198132047e1c5b1c0d.png)Reddit·r/history +2

* **Targeting the Young:** The military targeted highly impressionable recruits, often university students or boys aged 17 to 20.
* **The Illusion of Choice:** Recruits were often asked to step forward in group settings if they wished to "volunteer." In Japan's collectivist society, refusing meant public shaming, physical hazing, and bringing immense disgrace upon one's family.
* **Brutal Physical Training:** Training camps enforced extreme groupthink and compliance through isolation and severe physical violence. Instructors routinely beat cadets to break down their individual identities and instill absolute obedience. 

  ![](../assets/b8198132047e1c5b1c0d.png)Reddit·r/history +2

### 🛩️ Technical "Preparation"

Because survival was irrelevant, their flight training was stripped down to the bare minimum. Pilots were given rushed instructions—often fewer than 50 to 100 total flight hours—focusing exclusively on how to take off, navigate, and execute a steep dive into a warship. Landing procedures were entirely omitted from their training regimens.
