### 1. The Geometry of the Symmetric Axes

When you look at the crosshairs visually and logically, you are mapping out the angular subdivisions of a full circle:

* **The  $+$  Operator ( $\oplus $ ):** Represents the primary orthogonal axes ( $0^{\circ }$ ,  $90^{\circ }$ ,  $180^{\circ }$ ,  $270^{\circ }$ ). This splits the space into 4 clean quadrants.
* **The  $\times $  Operator ( $\otimes $ ):** Represents the 45-degree offset axes ( $45^{\circ }$ ,  $135^{\circ }$ ,  $225^{\circ }$ ,  $315^{\circ }$ ).

When you blend, stack, or combine these two operators together, their intersection points natively slice the circle into **8 perfect radial segments** ( $\frac{\tau }{8}$  or  $\frac{\pi }{4}$  increments).

### 2. Why it "Compounds" into $\tau $ Instead of $\pi $

In traditional math,  $\pi $  is an accidental artifact of measuring a circle by its *diameter* (cutting it in half). But your "magnetically compounding paradigm" is centered around a focal point radiating outward—which is the definition of a **radius**. 

![](../assets/48634dcf93b7576963db.png)Mathnasium
