The infinite monkey theorem states that a monkey hitting random keys on a typewriter for an infinite amount of time will eventually write the complete works of William Shakespeare. 

![](../assets/dd274eb8ec926e4ea610.png)Priceonomics

### What is the Theorem?

* **The Core Idea:** It is a famous thought experiment that explains probability, randomness, and the nature of infinity.
* **The Setup:** If you give a monkey a typewriter and an infinite amount of time—or use an infinite number of monkeys—they will eventually press every key combination possible. This means they will accidentally type every book ever written, including all of Shakespeare's plays. 

  ![](../assets/c96cc12df8388e1a4b59.png)BBC +3

### Why It Fails in the Real World

* **A Recent Study:** Mathematicians Stephen Woodcock and Jay Falletta from the University of Technology Sydney tested the math of the theorem in a 2024 study. 

  ![](../assets/641e9974433da6c48644.png)globalpost.com +1
* **Finite Resources:** They looked at the real global chimpanzee population (roughly 200,000) typing until the predicted end of the universe via heat death (about a googol, or 10¹⁰⁰, years). 

  ![](../assets/1d57d6444ab0fe1f6225.png)The Guardian +1
* **The Real Odds:** A single monkey typing for its entire life has only a 5% chance to randomly spell the simple word "banana". The odds of typing out Shakespeare's 884,647 words are so low that it will not happen before our universe collapses. 

  ![](../assets/e421a9daf1ebd712bab3.png)TikTok·ri\_science +1

### In Popular Culture

* **The Simpsons:** Mr. Burns famously says, "This is 1,000 monkeys working at 1,000 typewriters. Soon they'll have written the greatest novel known to man".
* **Real Trials:** Actual tests with captive macaques failed to write books, producing only five pages of text—mostly the letter "S"—along with damaging the keyboard. 

  ![](../assets/ad0acfcd6ef79b8b5c12.png)NPR +2

When discussing **finite resources**, the concept changes entirely depending on whether you are looking at it through the lens of math and probability (like the monkey theorem) or real-world sustainability and economics.

Here is how finite resources constrain our world across different fields.

---

### 1. Real-World Sustainability (Natural Resources)

In economics and environmental science, a finite resource (or [non-renewable resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-renewable_resource)) is a natural asset that **cannot be replenished at the speed it is consumed**.

* **Fossil Fuels:** Petroleum, coal, and natural gas take millions of years of heat and pressure to form. Once extracted and burned, they are gone for good.
* **Rare Earth Elements & Minerals:** Materials like lithium, cobalt, and copper are essential for modern electronics and electric car batteries. Their physical supply in the Earth's crust is strictly limited.
* **The Economic Impact:** Because these resources are limited, scarcity drives competition, geopolitics, and price volatility, forcing humanity to pivot toward renewable alternatives (like solar and wind).

---

### 2. Mathematics and Computing (The Monkey Constraint)

As mentioned with the infinite monkey theorem, switching from *infinite* to *finite* resources completely breaks down certain mathematical certainties.

* **Time Limitations:** In theory, infinity guarantees that any event with a probability greater than zero will happen. In reality, the universe has a time limit (estimated heat death).
* **Computational Boundaries:** In computer science, we always operate under finite resources—namely **CPU processing power, memory (RAM), and storage**. Algorithms must be optimized because we do not have infinite time or hardware to brute-force complex problems.

---

### Direct Comparison: Infinite vs. Finite Resources

| Feature | Infinite Resources (Theoretical) | Finite Resources (Real World) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Availability** | Unlimited time, space, and materials. | Restricted quantities; subject to depletion. |
| **Probability** | Improbable events are **guaranteed** to happen. | Improbable events are **functionally impossible**. |
| **Core Problem** | None (everything is possible). | **Scarcity** (requires budgeting and allocation). |
| **Examples** | Mathematical proofs, theoretical physics. | Fossil fuels, clean water, computer RAM, human lifespans. |
