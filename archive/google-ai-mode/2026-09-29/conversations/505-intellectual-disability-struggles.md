* **Emotional Impact:** Seeing someone face daily barriers with communication, self-care, or social inclusion often triggers feelings of grief, empathy, helplessness, or frustration.
* **Terminology Note:** The clinical and preferred modern term is **intellectual disability** (ID) rather than "mental retardation," which is outdated and often considered derogatory.
* **Severity Levels:** Intellectual disability ranges from mild to moderate, severe, and profound, affecting how independently a person can navigate daily life.
* **Support Available:** Specialized resources—such as occupational therapy, behavioral support, social services, and family counseling—help reduce daily struggles and improve quality of life for both the individual and their support network.

Here is what might actually be happening medically when someone presents with the struggles you described:

### 1. The "Dreamworld" and Psychosis (Schizophrenia)

When someone seems to live in an entirely different reality or a "dreamworld," they may be experiencing **psychosis**.

* **Delusions and Hallucinations:** In conditions like schizophrenia, a person’s brain processes sensory data incorrectly. They might see, hear, or firmly believe things that do not match shared reality.
* **Disorganization:** This can severely fracture their perception of time (**chronotemporal**) and their physical surroundings (**spatial**), making the world seem terrifyingly chaotic or completely unrecognizable to them.

### 2. "Reduced Facilities" (Intellectual & Developmental Disabilities)

When severe psychiatric conditions coexist with a lack of cognitive development, it is known as a **dual diagnosis**.

* **Cognitive Regression or Arrest:** If a person has a severe intellectual disability, their cognitive capacity and problem-solving skills may remain at the level of a toddler or young child.
* **Lack of Affordances:** "Affordances" are the clues the environment gives us on how to interact with it (e.g., knowing a handle means "pull"). For someone with both severe cognitive delays and psychosis, the environment loses all its meaning. They literally lack the tools, knowledge, and mental facilities to interact with the world safely.

### 3. Neurodevelopmental and Organic Brain Disorders

Sometimes, what looks like schizophrenia mixed with a childhood developmental state is actually a specific neurodevelopmental disorder or organic brain damage:

* **Severe Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD):** Can include profound communication barriers, intense spatial/sensory processing issues, and behaviors that look like a total withdrawal into an inner world.
* **Childhood Disintegrative Disorder / Rare Syndromes:** Conditions where a child develops normally for a few years and then experiences a severe loss of social, communication, and motor skills.
* **Organic Psychosis:** Brain injuries, severe chronic infections, or genetic conditions that physically alter brain structure, impairing both intellect and reality testing.

### Coping with the Emotional Toll

Watching a human being live with this level of cognitive and psychological fragmentation is a heavy burden. It triggers **compassion fatigue** and a unique kind of grief—mourning the independence and life that person cannot have.

People in this state require highly specialized, comprehensive care (neurologists, psychiatrists, and 24/7 supportive environments) to manage their confusion and keep them safe.
