Cordyceps is a genus of parasitic fungi known for infecting insects and arthropods, which has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese medicine. In the modern wellness space, it is widely consumed as a dietary supplement for energy, athletic performance, and immune support. 

💡 Key Benefits & Uses

While scientific research is ongoing and many studies have been limited to animals or laboratory settings, cordyceps is commonly used for: 
  • Athletic Performance: It is believed to increase the body's production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), which delivers energy to muscles and improves oxygen utilization during exercise. 
  • Immune System Support: It contains polysaccharides (like beta-glucans) that may help stimulate immune cells. 
  • Anti-Aging and Antioxidants: Traditional uses focus on reducing fatigue, boosting strength, and enhancing libido. 
  • Blood Sugar Regulation: Some studies suggest it may help keep blood sugar levels within a healthy range by mimicking the action of insulin. 

🔬 Common Species in Supplements

  1. Cordyceps sinensis: The rare, wild variety that grows on caterpillars in high-altitude regions of Tibet and the Himalayas. Because it is extremely expensive, it is rarely found in commercial supplements. 
  2. Cordyceps militaris: A species that can be commercially cultivated on grains or liquid substrates. It is affordable, highly potent, and the standard form used in most extracts, powders, and capsules today. 

⚠️ Potential Side Effects & Safety

Cordyceps is generally well-tolerated by most people, but you should keep the following in mind: 
  • Mild Digestive Issues: Some users report temporary nausea, dry mouth, or diarrhea.
  • Autoimmune Conditions: Because it stimulates the immune system, individuals with conditions like lupus, multiple sclerosis, or rheumatoid arthritis should avoid it unless advised by a doctor.
  • Bleeding Disorders: It may slow blood clotting. Stop taking it at least two weeks before any scheduled surgery.
  • Medication Interactions: It can interact with blood thinners and immunosuppressants.
