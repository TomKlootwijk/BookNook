The official biohazard symbol is a distinctive three-lobed trefoil design (☣) developed in 1966 by Charles Baldwin, an environmental-health engineer for Dow Chemical Company.

Key Standards and Design

  • Creation: Designed to be universally recognizable, symmetrical, and completely without prior cultural or commercial meaning.
  • Colors: Official regulations (such as OSHA) require the symbol to appear in a contrasting color (typically black) set against a fluorescent orange or orange-red background for regulated waste and infectious materials.
  • Global Standard: Adopted globally by agencies like the CDC, OSHA, and formalized as an international standard by the ISO in 1986.
  • Unicode: Coded digitally as U+2623.

Response 2 · Google AI Mode
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) does not classify the biohazard symbol (U+2623) itself by a "pandemic risk factor." Instead, the CDC and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) categorize biological agents and infectious diseases into four distinct Biosafety Levels (BSL) based on their risk to public health and the environment: 
ResearchGate +1
Biosafety LevelRisk LevelDescription & Pandemic Potential
BSL-1Minimal RiskMicroorganisms that do not consistently cause disease in healthy adults (e.g., non-pathogenic E. coli). No pandemic potential.
BSL-2Moderate RiskAgents associated with human disease, usually posing moderate hazard (e.g., Salmonella, Hepatitis B). Low pandemic potential; treatment is readily available.
BSL-3High RiskIndigenous or exotic agents that can cause serious or potentially lethal disease through respiratory transmission (e.g., Tuberculosis, SARS-CoV-2). Potential for regional or widespread epidemics.
BSL-4Extreme RiskDangerous or exotic agents that pose a high individual risk of life-threatening disease, which are frequently fatal and have no treatment/vaccine (e.g., Ebola virus, Marburg virus). Highest pandemic risk factor.

Key Regulatory Context

The biohazard symbol acts strictly as a point-of-contact warning on doors, equipment, and waste containment to ensure handlers take containment precautions. The level of danger is determined by the specific BSL protocol assigned to that containment space rather than the symbol itself.
