Response 3 · Google AI Mode
Exfoliating is a great step to clear out whiteheads and blackheads, as these are typically caused by trapped dead skin cells and oil. 
Since it has been a long time since you last exfoliated, the golden rule is to start slow and gentle. Over-exfoliating can irritate your skin and actually make breakouts worse. 

1. Choose a "Chemical" Exfoliant over a Scrub

Instead of a gritty face scrub (which can tear the skin and spread bacteria), look for a gentle leave-on liquid or serum containing Salicylic Acid (BHA). 
  • Why it works: Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, meaning it can deep-dive into your pores to dissolve the "glue" holding the whiteheads and blackheads together.
  • How to use it: Start by applying it just 2 evenings a week after washing your face. 

2. Build a Simple 3-Step Routine

You don't need a 10-step routine to see results. Stick to a basic, consistent routine: 
  • Morning:
    • Cleanse: Wash with water or a gentle cleanser.
    • Protect: Apply a lightweight SPF 30+ sunscreen (exfoliating makes your skin more sensitive to the sun). 
  • Evening:
    • Cleanse: Use a gentle, hydrating cleanser to remove the day's oil and dirt.
    • Exfoliate: Apply your Salicylic Acid product (only 2 nights a week to start).
    • Moisturize: Apply a lightweight, oil-free moisturizer to keep your skin barrier healthy. 

3. What to Expect

Give your skin 4 to 6 weeks to adapt. You might notice a temporary increase in breakouts at first—this is called "purging," where the exfoliant speeds up the clearing of clogs that were already forming under the surface.

  • Appearance: Clear, milky white, or off-white.

  • Odor: Mild or completely odorless. 
    Cleveland Clinic +2

2. Signs of a Potential Infection

If the texture changes significantly or is accompanied by irritation, it usually points to a highly treatable issue: 
  • Thick, clumpy, white "gunk" (like cottage cheese): This is a classic sign of a yeast infection (thrush). It is often accompanied by intense itching, redness, or burning. 
    nhs.uk +1
  • Thin, grayish-white fluid with a strong fishy smell: This typically indicates bacterial vaginosis (BV), which happens when the natural balance of bacteria changes. The smell is usually most noticeable after sex or during your period. 
    Cleveland Clinic +1
  • Green, frothy, or dark yellow discharge: This can be a sign of a sexually transmitted infection (STI), such as trichomoniasis, chlamydia, or gonorrhea. 
    Cleveland Clinic

Next Steps & Prevention

  • See a healthcare provider: If the fluid looks like cottage cheese, smells foul, or causes itching and pain, it's best to visit a doctor or a local clinic for a quick test so you can get the correct treatment. 
    Cleveland Clinic +1
  • Avoid "cleaning" inside: The vagina is self-cleaning. Avoid douching, scented soaps, or feminine wipes. These strip away the healthy bacteria and actually cause or worsen infections. Washing the outside (the vulva) with just warm water is all you need. 
    Flo.health +4
