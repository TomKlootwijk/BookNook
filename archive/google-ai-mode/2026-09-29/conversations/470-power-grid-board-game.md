### Core Gameplay Mechanics

The game is famous for its tight mathematical balancing and lack of luck. It plays out across three interconnected phases every round:

* **The Auction:** Players bid against each other to buy increasingly efficient power plants (ranging from old-school coal and oil burners to green wind turbines and nuclear plants).
* **Resource Management:** Players buy fuel (coal, oil, garbage, or uranium) from a dynamic market. As a resource is bought up, its price goes up for the remaining players, simulating real-world supply and demand.
* **Network Building:** Players pay to connect cities on the map to their power grids.

### The Catch-Up Mechanic

One of *Power Grid's* most famous design choices is its aggressive catch-up mechanic. The player who is currently winning (the one with the most connected cities) is forced to go **last** when buying resources and building connections, meaning they pay the highest prices. Conversely, the player in last place gets first pick of cheap resources, keeping everyone in the race until the very end.

### Game Editions & Extensions

* **Power Grid Recharged:** The updated modern baseline edition featuring minor rule tweaks to streamline the plant market and improve component quality.
* **Expansion Maps:** The base game usually features the USA and Germany maps. Dozens of expansions exist offering alternative maps—like Europe, Asia, and South America—each introducing unique regional economic rules.
* **Power Grid: The Card Game:** A standalone, faster-playing version that distills the auction and resource management mechanics into under an hour by removing the map board entirely.
