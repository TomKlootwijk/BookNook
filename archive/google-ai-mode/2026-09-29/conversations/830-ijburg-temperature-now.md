Wasp colonies do not survive the winter. Their lifecycle is directly tied to autumn temperatures and the year’s first freezing conditions: 

![](../assets/00e91e3c02192b6142c5.png)www.waspsremoval.co.uk

### The Autumn Timeline

* **Mid-to-Late October:** As temperatures consistently drop below **10°C to 12°C (50°F)**, worker wasps lose their ability to fly effectively, stop foraging, and begin dying off in massive numbers. 

  ![](../assets/1f9cd22c1c7835fbe3d8.png)Lookout Pest Control +1
* **The First Hard Frost:** This is the absolute cutoff point. The first sustained freeze completely finishes off any remaining worker wasps, males, and the old queen, officially ending the nest. 

  ![](../assets/00e91e3c02192b6142c5.png)www.waspsremoval.co.uk +1
* **Mild Autumn Exceptions:** In unusually warm or mild years, active nests insulated inside structural spaces like warm attics or lofts can sometimes stretch their activity well into **November** or even early **December**. 

  ![](../assets/33f8ddf77d3aaae1050f.png)PEST UK +2

### Winter Sightings (The Exception)

If you spot a wasp in the dead of winter (December through February), it is **strictly a newly fertilized queen**. Queens are the only members of the colony that survive the cold by hibernating in sheltered spots like wall cavities, log piles, or loft insulation. If a home's central heating or indoor lighting accidentally warms her up, she may briefly wake up confused and crawl around a window or bedroom.
