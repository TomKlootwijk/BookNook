How a Waterpas Works

A water level consists of a long, clear hose filled with water, with both ends open to the atmosphere. 
  • The Principle: Because the ends are connected by the same body of water, it functions as a communicating vessel. Hydrostatic pressure ensures that the water surface at both ends settles at the exact same horizontal plane. 
  • The Benefit: Unlike a short spirit level, a water level allows you to transfer accurate level lines around corners, over obstacles, or across large rooms (e.g., 10 to 20 meters apart). 

How to Use a Water Level (Step-by-Step)

  1. Fill the hose: Use a clear hose and fill it almost completely with clean water. Ensure there are no air bubbles inside, as bubbles disrupt the accuracy. 
  2. Hold the ends together: Keep both ends of the hose next to each other. The water lines should align perfectly. Mark this baseline. 
  3. Position the first end: Hold one end against your reference height mark on the first wall. 
  4. Move the second end: Stretch the hose out to the distant area or wall where you need the new mark. Hold the tube end up against the wall. 
  5. Let it settle: Wait for the water to stop moving. Once steady, align the water level at the first end exactly with your reference mark. 
  6. Mark the line: The water level at the second end now indicates the exact same horizontal height. Mark it on the second wall.

The "Hydrostatic Waterpas" in Modern 3D Extrusion

In large-scale industrial 3D printing (such as extruding carbon fiber composites or concrete), maintaining a perfectly level bed over a vast area is critical. Traditional spirit levels fail at this scale. Instead, systems use an automated hydrostatic leveling network—essentially a high-tech waterpas. 
  • Hydrostatic Precision: Open fluid reservoirs are placed at the corners of the 3D printer bed, connected by a network of tubes. Just like the manual waterpas, hydrostatic pressure ensures the liquid surface across all reservoirs sits on the exact same horizontal plane. 
  • Electrolytic/Conductive Sensing: To turn this physical liquid level into digital data for the 3D printer, the system uses an electrical current. By using a conductive liquid (or an electrolyte) and a carbon probe, the system measures electrical resistance or capacitance when the probe touches the liquid surface. 
  • 3D Extrusion Adjustment: The 3D printer's computer reads these precise micro-measurements. If one corner of the bed expands or warps due to the heat of carbon extrusion, the printer instantly micro-adjusts its Z-axis extrusion nozzle to perfectly mirror ("carbon copy") the true hydrostatic level line.

You are describing circuit painting, a popular method for prototyping and educational electronics where conductive ink—like Bare Conductive Electric Paint—is used to draw circuit traces directly onto paper, plastic, or 3D-printed surfaces instead of using traditional copper wires. 
When you connect these painted traces to a Microcontroller Unit (MCU), like an Arduino, Raspberry Pi Pico, or ESP32, you can turn your painted designs into interactive, touch-sensitive interfaces. 

How Circuit Painting Connects to an MCU

 [ Painted Touch Pad ] --------( Painted Trace )--------> [ MCU Input Pin ]
                                                            (e.g., Arduino)
  1. The Conductive Ink: Materials like Bare Conductive paint are carbon-based and conductive. While they have a higher resistance than copper wire, they easily carry low-voltage signals over short distances. 
  2. Capacitive Touch Sensing: Most modern MCUs have built-in capacitive touch pins. When you connect a painted trace to one of these pins, the MCU can detect the electrical change (capacitance) that happens when a human finger touches the paint. 
  3. The Connection Interface: To bridge the gap between the painted paper and the MCU pins, developers usually use alligator clips, conductive copper tape, or small metal rivets/through-holes clamped directly onto the dried paint. 

Key Technical Considerations

  • Resistance: Carbon-based conductive paint has a noticeable amount of resistance (Bare Conductive is roughly 55 ohms per square at 50 microns layer thickness). Keep your painted traces wide and short to minimize power loss. 
  • Sealing: Once the paint dries, it remains water-soluble. To protect your circuit from smudging or shorting out from sweaty fingers, it should be sealed with a clear acrylic spray or varnish after it completely dries.
