A pig butchering scam (derived from the Chinese phrase shā zhū pán) is a highly sophisticated, long-con financial fraud that combines online romance, emotional manipulation, and fraudulent cryptocurrency investments. The name refers to the agricultural practice of "fattening a pig" (building trust and intimacy) before sending it to "slaughter" (stealing the victim's life savings). 
Wikipedia +2
According to FBI and global data, it has escalated into a massive threat, contributing to tens of billions of dollars in losses worldwide. Many of the frontline "scammers" sending these messages are themselves human trafficking victims held against their will by transnational organized crime syndicates in Southeast Asia. 
YouTube·PBS NewsHour +2

🕒 How the Scam Works (The 4 Phases)

  1. The "Wrong Number" or Cold Contact: The scammer initiates contact through WhatsApp, SMS, LinkedIn, Tinder, or Instagram. A common opening line is an innocent mistake: "Oh, sorry! I thought this was my golf coach." 
    TRM Labs +2
  2. The Grooming Phase: If you reply, they steer the conversation into a friendly or romantic relationship. They spend weeks or even months building deep trust, sending daily check-ins, mirroring your interests, and showing off a wealthy lifestyle. 
    The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (.gov) +2
  3. The Investment Pitch: They casually mention making massive passive income trading cryptocurrency or foreign assets, guided by an "expert uncle" or proprietary insider algorithm. They safely guide you to buy real crypto on a legitimate platform (like Coinbase or Kraken) but then tell you to transfer it to a specific, fraudulent trading website or app. 
    The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (.gov) +3
  4. The False Sense of Security: The fake platform displays artificial gains. They may even allow you to withdraw a small amount of money early on to prove it is "safe." Intrigued, victims invest larger sums, sometimes liquidating retirement funds or taking out loans. 
    YouTube·BBC World Service +2
  5. The Slaughter: When you try to withdraw your total balance, the platform blocks you. They claim you must first pay massive "IRS taxes," "VIP clearance fees," or "withdrawal deposits." Once you refuse or run out of money, the scammer completely vanishes and deletes their accounts. 
    The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (.gov) +3

🚨 Major Red Flags to Watch For

  • The Quick Shift to Encrypted Apps: They immediately try to move you from a dating or social platform over to WhatsApp or Telegram.
  • They Always Dodge Video Calls: They look like attractive models in their pictures but will provide endless, elaborate excuses for why they cannot meet in person or do a live video call.
  • Unsolicited Investment Advice: An online-only friend or romantic interest shouldn't be giving you financial advice or pressuring you to invest.
  • Unrealistic, Guaranteed Returns: Any platform promising zero risk and extraordinarily high financial returns is fraudulent.
  • Demands for Extra Cash to Cash Out: Regulated platforms never demand upfront fees or taxes in crypto to unlock a standard withdrawal. 
    United States Secret Service (.gov) +7

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself

  • Never take financial advice from someone you have never met in person.
  • Do not use URLs or download apps sent by strangers. Always type official addresses into your browser manually.
  • Conduct a reverse-image search on the person's profile pictures to check if they are using stolen photos.
  • Be skeptical of random text messages. If an unknown number texts you by "mistake," ignore it and block the number. 
    Community First Bank | Wisconsin Community Banking +2
If you or someone you know has fallen victim to this, immediately stop sending money. Document all transaction IDs, wallet addresses, and chat logs, and report the fraud to your bank and local cybercrime authorities, such as the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) or the Federal Trade Commission (FTC).
