Response 1 · Google AI Mode
Depending on the exact context of the situation, there are a few different terms for this behavior. 
The most common terms used when a gambler continues to bet despite knowing they are likely to lose include: 
  • Chasing Losses: This is the most accurate term for when a gambler continues to bet to try and win back money they have already lost. Even when they logically know the odds are against them and they will likely lose more, the emotional urge to "get back to zero" overrides logic. It is a primary warning sign of a gambling disorder. 
  • The Sunk Cost Fallacy: This is a psychological term where a person continues a behavior because they have already invested time, effort, or money into it. The gambler reasons, "I've already lost $500, if I stop now that money is gone forever, so I have to keep playing," even though continuing means risking more losses.

  • The Gambler's Fallacy: This occurs when a player wrongly believes that a certain outcome is "due" to happen. For example, if a roulette wheel hits black five times in a row, a gambler might confidently bet on red because they feel it must win next, completely ignoring that the mathematical odds remain exactly the same on every single spin. 
  • Tilt / Being on Tilt: Originating in poker but used across many types of gambling and gaming, this describes a state of mental or emotional frustration where a player begins making terrible, aggressive bets that they know are bad because they are upset about prior losses. 
If you are looking for a term where someone is intentionally losing on purpose (such as throwing a match or a game), that is usually referred to as hustling, dumping, or match-fixing. 
Are you looking for the term from a psychological/addiction standpoint, or are you looking for slang used at a casino table or poker game?
