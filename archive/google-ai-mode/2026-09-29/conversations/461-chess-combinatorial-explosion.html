In the game of chess, combinatorial explosion refers to the astronomical, exponential growth of possible game paths that occurs with each consecutive move. Because a standard chess position offers a high branching factor—meaning a player has an average of 20 to 40 legal moves available at any given turn—the resulting "game tree" quickly multiplies out of control. 
Oxford Reference +1
This mathematical reality makes it entirely impossible for any computer to calculate chess by raw brute-force from start to finish. 
Oxford Reference

The Exponential Scale of Chess

To understand how quickly the numbers explode, consider how a game progresses from the very first move: 
  • Move 1 (White then Black): White has 20 options, and Black has 20 replies. This yields 400 possible board positions.
  • Move 2: The number jumps to 8,902 positions.
  • Move 3: There are over 121 million possible positions.
  • Move 4: The variations soar to nearly 318 billion paths.
  • Move 40: The total number of unique game paths explodes to approximately 10⁴⁰, a number roughly comparable to the total number of atoms in the observable universe. 
    LinkedIn +1

The Shannon Number 

In 1950, mathematician and information theorist Claude Shannon published a groundbreaking paper estimating the total number of unique legal chess game sequences to be roughly 10¹²⁰. Known as the Shannon Number, this figure is vastly larger than the estimated number of atoms in the observable universe (around 10⁸⁰). 
Facebook
If a supercomputer could analyze one billion (10⁹) chess sequences every single second, it would still take over 10,000 years just to look a mere 15 moves ahead from a single position.

How Humans and Computers Defuse the Explosion

Because exhaustive calculation is impossible, both artificial intelligence and human players have had to develop brilliant shortcuts to navigate the game.

MethodHow it Manages the Explosion
Alpha-Beta PruningAn AI search algorithm that cuts off branches of the game tree as soon as it realizes a move leads to a worse position than a previously analyzed option, drastically reducing the math.
