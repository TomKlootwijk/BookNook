Response 1 · Google AI Mode
In linguistic terms, -ology (or -logy) is itself a suffix, which means it attaches to the end of a root word. 
Wikipedia
However, since you mentioned oligopoly, you are looking for how -ology interacts with other prefixes and suffixes, or words where -ology sits in the middle. The word oligopoly actually combines the prefix oligo- (few) with the suffix -poly (sell), so it doesn't contain -ology (the study of). 
Depending on what you are trying to build or look up, here is how you can add prefixes and suffixes to -ology words: 

1. Suffixes added to -ology

When you add a suffix to an -ology word, you change its part of speech (e.g., from a field of study to a person or an adjective): 
  • -ist (forms a noun for a person): Turns the study into the specialist who practices it.
    • Examples: Biologist, Psychologist, Dermatologist. 
      Vocabulary.com +1
  • -ical / -ic (forms an adjective): Turns the study into a descriptive word.
    • Examples: Biological, Psychological, Technological. 
  • -ically (forms an adverb): Describes how an action is performed.
    • Examples: Biologically, Chronologically. 

2. Prefixes added to -ology words

You can attach standard English prefixes to the front of existing -ology words to alter their meaning: 
  • Bio- (Life): Biology (The study of life).
  • Psych- (Mind): Psychology (The study of the mind).
  • Geo- (Earth): Geology (The study of the earth).
  • Neuro- (Nerve): Neurology (The study of the nervous system).
  • Path- (Disease): Pathology (The study of diseases).
  • Eco- (Environment): Ecology (The study of environment relationships).
  • Micro- (Small) + Bio-: Microbiology (The study of microscopic organisms).
  • Pseudo- (False): Pseudology (The study or practice of lying). 
    Study.com +3

3. Words containing both a Prefix and a Suffix around -logy

If you are looking for complex words that sandwich a root between an opening prefix and a closing suffix (similar to the structure of oligopoly): 
  • An- (Without) + esthesi- + -ology: Anesthesiology
  • E- (Out) + tym- + -ology: Etymology
  • Archaeo- (Ancient) + -logist: Archaeologist
