### 🚫 What NOT to Do (The Myths)

* **Do NOT pee on it:** This is a famous myth, but urine can actually cause the stinging cells to release *more* venom.
* **Do NOT use fresh water or alcohol:** This triggers the remaining stinging cells to fire, making the pain worse.
* **Do NOT rub the area:** Do not rub it with sand, towels, or your hands.

### 🩹 Exactly What to Do If Stung

If you or someone else gets stung, follow these steps immediately:

1. **Rinse with ocean water:** Carefully rinse the sting with plenty of saltwater (from the sea, not fresh water) to wash away loose tentacles.
2. **Remove remaining tentacles:** Use a pair of tweezers, a stick, or a gloved hand to carefully lift any remaining tentacles off the skin. Do **not** touch them with your bare fingers.
3. **Soak in hot water:** Immerse the affected skin in hot water—ideally between **42°C to 45°C (107°F to 113°F)**—or apply a hot pack for 20 to 45 minutes. The heat helps break down the toxins and relieve the intense pain.
4. **Use ice if hot water isn't available:** If you cannot get hot water, using a cold pack or ice wrapped in a dry cloth can help numb the area.

### 🚨 When to Seek Emergency Medical Attention

Call emergency services immediately if:

* The sting covers a **large area of the body** (like the entire chest, back, or neck).
* The person shows signs of a **severe allergic reaction** (difficulty breathing, swelling of the throat or tongue, dizziness, or chest pain).
