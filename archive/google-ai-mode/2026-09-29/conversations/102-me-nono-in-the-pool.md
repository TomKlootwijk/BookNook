**No poop in the pool water** is the absolute golden rule of swimming!

Keeping fecal matter out of the swimming pool fluid is crucial for health and safety. According to the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/prevention/preventing-diarrheal-illnesses.html), public and backyard pools can easily be contaminated by microscopic particles or accidental leaks. 

![](../assets/e05ad42b0da4f59d37d9.png)Nationwide Children's Hospital

### 🦠 Why it Matters

When feces enters the water, it introduces dangerous bacteria, viruses, and parasites. The most problematic is **Cryptosporidium** (or "Crypto"), a chlorine-resistant parasite that can survive in a properly treated pool for **more than 7 days**. Swallowing even a tiny drop of contaminated pool water can cause severe diarrheal illness, stomach cramps, and dehydration. 

![](../assets/e05ad42b0da4f59d37d9.png)Nationwide Children's Hospital

### 🏊‍♂️ Gold Rules for Swimmers

To keep the pool fluid clean and safe for everyone, health experts recommend these essential habits:

* **Never swim with diarrhea:** If you or a child are sick, stay completely out of the water. If diagnosed with Crypto, wait at least **two weeks** after diarrhea stops before returning to the pool. 

  ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov) +1
* **Shower before jumping in:** A quick **one-minute shower** rinses off dirt, sweat, and the invisible particles on your body that eat up the pool's chlorine. 

  ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov)
* **Take hourly bathroom breaks:** Proactively take children to the restroom every 30 to 60 minutes so accidents don't happen in the water. 

  ![](../assets/e05ad42b0da4f59d37d9.png)Nationwide Children's Hospital
* **Check swim diapers often:** Keep in mind that swim diapers are *not* leak-proof. They only delay poop from entering the water for a few minutes. Always change diapers in a designated restroom away from the pool area. 

  ![](../assets/87c001cab2d7e5d11222.png)Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | CDC (.gov) +1
* **Do not swallow pool water:** Avoid getting pool water in your mouth to protect yourself from any lingering germs. 

  ![](../assets/88c091012945c32406fe.png)NBC News +1

If an accident does happen, pool operators must immediately evacuate the area and execute a rigorous **hyperchlorination protocol** to fully sanitize the fluid before anyone can safely swim again.
