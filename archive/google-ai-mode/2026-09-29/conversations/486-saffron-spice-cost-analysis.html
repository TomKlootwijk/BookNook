I think the most expensive spice is saffron
Response 1 · Google AI Mode
Yes, saffron is widely recognized as the world's most expensive spice, often costing anywhere from $500 to $10,000 per pound (roughly $1,100 to $22,000 per kilogram) depending on its quality and grade. 
YouTube·Simple Things - Surprising Histories +2

Why Saffron Is So Expensive

  • Hand-Harvested: Saffron comes from the delicate red stigmas of the Crocus sativus (saffron crocus) flower. Each flower produces only three tiny stigmas, which must be picked completely by hand.
  • Massive Labor Yield: It takes roughly 75,000 to 150,000 flowers to produce just one pound of dried saffron.
  • Short Harvest Window: The flowers bloom for only a brief two-week window in the autumn, and the harvest cannot be automated. 
    YouTube·History of Simple Things +2

Other Top Expensive Spices

  • Vanilla: Frequently ranked as the second most expensive spice in the world due to its labor-intensive hand-pollination process.
  • Cardamom: Often called the "Queen of Spices," it rounds out the top tier of high-value culinary spices. 
    gastropod.com +1
Companies like Rumi Spice source hand-picked threads directly from local harvesters, reflecting the intense labor required behind every gram.

Prompt 2 · You
If you need tips on how to spot real saffron versus fake substitutes yes
Response 2 · Google AI Mode
You can easily spot real saffron from fake substitutes—like dyed corn silk, safflower petals, or shredded paper—using a few quick sensory and water tests. Counterfeiters often try to replicate "red gold," but real saffron has chemical properties they cannot easily fake. 
Kashmiril +3

1. The Cold Water Test (Most Reliable)

Drop 3 to 5 threads into a small glass of room-temperature or warm water and observe how the color bleeds: 
Kashmiril +1
  • Real Saffron: It is patient. It will slowly release a clear, golden-yellow hue over 10 to 15 minutes. Crucially, the threads will stay deep red even after soaking. 
    Instagram +2
  • Fake Saffron: It bleeds aggressively. It will dump dark red or bright orange artificial dye within seconds. After a short time, the fake threads will bleach and turn completely pale, white, or translucent. 
    Saffron Market Australia +3

2. The Taste and Smell Tests

  • The Smell: Real saffron has a highly distinct aroma that smells like a blend of sweet honey and dry hay, sometimes with a faint metallic or earthy edge. Fake saffron often smells like absolutely nothing, or it smells chemically or unnaturally perfumed. 
    Kashmiril +2
  • The Taste: Place a single thread on your tongue. Real saffron always tastes slightly bitter and astringent. If it tastes sweet, the vendor has likely soaked fake fibers (or real low-grade saffron) in sugar water or syrup to artificially add weight to the package. 
    Instagram +3

3. Physical Appearance and Texture

  • The Shape: Look closely at the strands. Authentic saffron threads are trumpet-shaped—they flare out to be wider and bell-shaped at the top, and narrow down at the bottom. Fake saffron (like cut silk or paper) usually looks perfectly uniform, straight, and flat like a basic piece of string.
  • The Touch: Rub a soaked saffron thread between your thumb and forefinger. Real saffron threads are durable and will remain completely intact without shredding. It will also leave a distinct yellowish-orange stain on your skin. Fake threads will immediately crumble, disintegrate, or turn slimy. 
    YouTube·Learning Fever +5

4. The Baking Soda Trick

If you want a fun chemical test, mix a tiny pinch of baking soda into a small amount of water, then drop in your threads. If the saffron is pure, the alkaline environment will turn the water a golden or pale greenish-yellow. If it is fake or artificially dyed, the liquid will turn dark red or won't change color at all. 
Kashmiril +1
A Golden Rule: Always avoid buying pre-ground saffron powder. Counterfeiters easily pad saffron powder with cheap fillers like turmeric, paprika, or dyed rice flour, making it nearly impossible to authenticate without a lab. Stick to whole threads from trusted, transparent brands like Heray Spice or Rumi Spice. 
Mother Would Know +2

St. Lucia buns
—traditionally known as Lussekatter (Lucia cats) in Sweden—are a celebrated, sweet yeast pastry deeply woven into Nordic winter traditions. Baked specifically to honor Saint Lucia's Day on December 13th, these treats rely on a generous infusion of saffron to give them their distinct, radiant golden hue, symbolizing light triumphing over the dark winter solstice. 
ScandiKitchen +2
Steeped in rich folklore, the buns are traditionally shaped into an elegant "S" curve to mimic a curled-up cat's tail, with a single dark raisin nestled into the center of each spiral to represent the cat's eyes. Recipe Overview

  • Prep Time: 2 hours (includes rising time)
  • Cook Time: 10–12 minutes
  • Yield: 14–15 buns 
    Gelson's Markets +3

Ingredients

  • 1 gram saffron threads (ground or finely crushed)
  • ¾ cup (180 ml) whole milk
  • ¼ cup (60 grams) unsalted butter
  • ¼ cup sour cream or Greek yogurt (keeps the dough moist)
  • 1 sachet (7 grams / 1 tbsp) active dry yeast
  • ⅓ cup (70 grams) granulated sugar
  • 3 ½ cups (500 grams) strong white bread flour or all-purpose flour
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 2 large eggs (1 for the dough, 1 beaten with a splash of water for the egg wash)
  • 30 raisins 
    BBC +6

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Bloom the Saffron & Yeast: In a small saucepan over medium heat, warm the milk until steaming but not boiling. Remove from heat, stir in the crushed saffron, and let it steep for 10 minutes to draw out its golden color. Once the milk cools to lukewarm, stir in the yeast and a pinch of sugar. Let it sit for 5 minutes until frothy. 
    epicurious.com +2
  2. Make the Dough: Melt the butter and whisk it into the saffron-milk mixture along with the sour cream, sugar, salt, and 1 egg. Gradually add the flour, kneading by hand or with a stand mixer for about 10 minutes until you have a smooth, elastic, slightly sticky dough. 
    Scandinavian Cookbook +3
  3. First Rise: Place the dough in a greased bowl, cover with a kitchen towel, and let it rise in a warm place for 1 hour, or until it doubles in size. 
    bestrecipes.com.au +1
  4. Shape the "Cats": Punch down the dough and divide it into 14 to 15 equal portions. Roll each piece on a lightly floured surface into a rope about 12 inches (30 cm) long. Curl the left end tightly clockwise toward the center, and the right end counterclockwise toward the center to form a tight "S" shape. 
    lilvienna.com +3
  5. Second Rise: Place the shaped buns on parchment-lined baking sheets. Cover loosely and let them puff up for 30 to 45 minutes. Meanwhile, preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). 
    lilvienna.com +2
  6. Decorate & Bake: Press one raisin firmly into the center of each of the two spirals on each bun. Brush the tops gently with your beaten egg wash. Bake for 10 to 12 minutes until they are puffed and light golden brown. Let them cool on a wire rack under a clean kitchen towel to keep them soft. 
    lilvienna.com +5

Pro Tips

  • The Secret to Softness: Traditional Swedish bakers often add a bit of quark, sour cream, or skyr to the dough. Saffron can naturally dry out bread dough, so these ingredients add necessary fat and acidity to yield an incredibly soft and billowy bun. 
    True North Kitchen +3
  • Plump the Raisins: Soak your raisins in warm water for a few minutes and pat them dry before pressing them into the dough. This prevents the raisins from burning or absorbing moisture out of the surrounding bun while baking. 
    lilvienna.com +1
