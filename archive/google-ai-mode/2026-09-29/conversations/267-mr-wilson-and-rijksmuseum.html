Here is how that poetic concept translates into technical computer graphics principles: 

1. Ray Tracing and Global Illumination

To render a realistic water droplet, a CG artist relies on Ray Tracing. Instead of just painting a surface, the computer simulates millions of individual light rays. 
  • Refraction (Snell's Law): Light passes through the droplet, bending based on the refractive index of water (1.333). This flips and distorts the entire background world, compressing it inside the droplet's volume. 
  • Total Internal Reflection: Light rays bounce around inside the sphere before exiting, capturing multiple angles of the digital environment. 

2. Environment Mapping (HDRI)

In VFX, a single image can contain an entire world. To light digital assets dynamically, TDs (Technical Directors) use an HDRI (High Dynamic Range Image) environment map, which is often formatted as a spherical texture map.
If you project a full 360-degree environment map onto a tiny, highly reflective, refractive sphere—like a water droplet—the entire environment is geometrically mapped, warped, and stored inside that tiny visual space. A digital "capsule of time," quite literally. 

3. The Math of a Droplet Projection

Mathematically, a spherical droplet maps a 3D environment (X, Y, Z) into a compressed 2D coordinate system (U, V) on its surface.
