The exhausting and dissipation rates of gaseous emissions from geothermal and hydrothermal vents depend primarily on depth, fluid buoyancy, and local hydrodynamic currents. While individual vent emission rates vary widely—from small flows of a few liters per minute to massive discharges exceeding 23 moles of CO₂ per second at active sites—their physical dissipation occurs through a highly dynamic, two-stage fluid plume structure. 
Harvard University +2

The Two-Stage Dissipation Process

1. The Buoyant Plume Phase (Rapid Turbulent Dilution) 

When superheated fluids (reaching temperatures from 40°C up to 400°C) are forcefully expelled from the Earth's crust into the cold, dense ocean depths (~4°C), they possess a drastically lower density. This density differential triggers a powerful vertical surge: 
Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution +1
  • Turbulent Shear: As the gaseous fluid column rapidly jets upwards, severe shear forces develop between the hot plume and the stagnant surrounding seawater. 
    Wikiwand
  • Entrainment: This turbulence causes ambient ocean water to be violently drawn into the plume, leading to massive, exponential dilution within the first few tens to hundreds of meters above the seabed. 
    Wikiwand

2. The Non-Buoyant Plume Phase (Horizontal Advection and Lateral Dispersion) 

As the ascending plume continually entrains freezing seawater, it rapidly cools and gains density. 
Wikiwand
  • Neutral Buoyancy Point: Eventually, the density of the plume equals that of the surrounding ocean layers, halting its upward momentum.
  • Lateral Spreading: At this height (often 100 to 400 meters above the seafloor), the plume spreads laterally into a massive horizontal sheet. Gases like methane (CH₄), carbon dioxide (CO₂), and hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) are then slowly dissipated over hundreds of kilometers driven by ambient ocean currents (advection) and eddy diffusion. 
    ScienceDirect.com +3

Key Factors Governing Gaseous Dissipation Rates

FactorMechanismImpact on Dissipation
Depth & PressureHigh hydrostatic pressure in the deep ocean prevents gases from boiling or violently expanding into massive bubble phases.Confines dissipation to liquid-dissolved plume dynamics.
Vent Geometry (Discrete vs. Diffuse)Discrete "black smoker" vents jet focused fluids at high velocities. Diffuse flows leak cooler, pre-mixed fluids over vast areas.Discrete vents push gases high into the water column; diffuse flows dissipate gases slowly and closer to the seabed.
Biological ConsumptionChemosynthetic bacteria line the paths of these plumes, aggressively consuming H₂S and CH₄.Rapidly decreases the chemical concentration and alters the dissipation radius.
Shallow Vents vs. Deep VentsVents situated in shallow waters (<100m) or onshore cannot achieve neutral buoyancy before breaching the surface.Gases fail to dissolve or convert within the water column and exhaust directly into the atmosphere.
