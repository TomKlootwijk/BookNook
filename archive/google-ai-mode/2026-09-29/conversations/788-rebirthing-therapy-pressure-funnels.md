## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

The phrase you are describing refers to **"compression therapy"** or physical **"rebirthing therapy"**—a controversial, widely condemned, and scientifically unsupported practice. It is most heavily associated with the tragic **death of Candace Newmaker** in April 2000. 

![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +2

### The Core Mechanism & Intent

This specific practice originated as a subset of **attachment therapy**. Proponents claimed that if a child had failed to form a healthy bond with their parents (often diagnosed as Reactive Attachment Disorder), they could "reset" their psychological development by psychologically and physically re-enacting their own birth. 

![](../assets/cfdd644f88f5ced797fd.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +3

### The Specific Method

The process you described—resembling an intense funnel, tunnel, or birth canal through which the individual must crawl or push under heavy pressure—involved the following steps: 

![](../assets/2c5afe10de3355423ed2.png)Women's Congressional Policy Institute +1

* **The "Womb" Enclosure:** The individual (frequently a child) was wrapped tightly from head to toe in a thick flannel sheet or blanket. 

  ![](../assets/cfdd644f88f5ced797fd.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +1
* **Funnel/Tunnel Pressure:** The individual was surrounded by heavy pillows. Multiple adult facilitators would then use their bodies, hands, or feet to lean directly onto the pillows and sheets. This generated **several hundred pounds of physical pressure** to simulate uterine contractions. 

  ![](../assets/cfdd644f88f5ced797fd.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +3
* **The Escape Attempt:** The individual was instructed to fight, crawl, and push their way out of the tightly squeezed enclosure to be "born" into the arms of their adoptive parent. 

  ![](../assets/2b5a17bdbca7efc0ee3b.png)ABC News - Breaking News, Latest News and Videos +1

### The Medical and Legal Fallout

This physical simulation is **highly dangerous** and distinct from the separate, gentle breathing practices also known as "rebirthing breathwork". 

![](../assets/f0a86407bd5f496086c6.png)WebMD

During a 70-minute session in Colorado, 10-year-old Candace Newmaker repeatedly warned therapists that she could not breathe and felt she was going to die. The therapists ignored her pleas, assuming she was being "manipulative," and continued to apply pressure. She lost consciousness and **died of mechanical asphyxiation**. 

![](../assets/2b5a17bdbca7efc0ee3b.png)ABC News - Breaking News, Latest News and Videos +4

The two primary therapists involved were convicted of reckless child abuse resulting in death and sentenced to **16 years in prison**. The tragedy led to the passage of **Candace's Law**, which officially banned physical rebirthing and compression practices in Colorado and North Carolina, with the [U.S. Senate](https://diabetesjournals.org/diabetes/article/74/4/514/157403/IER3IP1-Mutations-Cause-Neonatal-Diabetes-Due-to) issuing a formal resolution condemning the practice nationwide.
