**Consuming homemade yogurt cultured from vaginal discharge or consuming the human placenta poses severe health risks and is strongly discouraged by medical professionals.** While some individuals attempt these practices due to misconceptions about health benefits or spiritual rituals, both practices expose you to dangerous pathogens and offer no proven medical advantages.

### Culturing Yogurt from Vaginal Discharge

While it is true that a healthy vagina contains *Lactobacillus*—the same genus of bacteria used to ferment commercial dairy products—attempting to make "yoghurt" at home using bodily fluids is highly unsafe: 

![](../assets/8b56a28a6499e12ca8b0.png)ScienceAlert +1

* **Pathogenic Contamination:** Vaginal secretions do not contain isolated, sterile strains of beneficial bacteria. They harbor a complex mix of microorganisms. Fermenting these at home can inadvertently cultivate harmful pathogens, such as *Group B Streptococcus*, *E. coli*, or sexually transmitted infections, leading to severe foodborne illness. 

  ![](../assets/28acff153d917ec6f458.png)CBS News +1
* **Ineffective Fermentation:** Microbiologists note that vaginal *Lactobacilli* are generally poor milk-sourers on their own and require specific environmental conditions and companion strains (like *Streptococcus thermophilus*) to safely create true yogurt.

### Consuming the Placenta (Placentophagy)

Treating the placenta as a "holy" or highly nutritious substance to be eaten (either raw, cooked, or encapsulated) carries significant clinical warnings from major health organizations, including the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)](https://diabetesjournals.org/diabetes/article/75/1/193/163844/Gestational-Diabetes-Mellitus-Alters-Placental):

* **Infection Risks:** The placenta acts as a protective filter during pregnancy, trapping toxins and bacteria to protect the fetus. Consuming it can reintroduce harmful bacteria (like *Salmonella* or *Group B Streptococcus*) into your body. This poses a direct life-threatening risk to a nursing infant if the mother passes a bloodborne infection through breast milk.
* **No Proven Benefit:** Rigorous scientific studies have found no evidence that placentophagy treats postpartum depression, boosts iron levels, or improves milk supply.

If you are looking to support your microbiome or support postpartum recovery, it is safest to rely on regulated, commercially prepared probiotics and standard medical care.
