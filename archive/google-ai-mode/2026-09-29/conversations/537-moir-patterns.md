A **moiré pattern** is a large-scale, secondary visual design created when two repeating geometric patterns—such as grids, parallel lines, or concentric circles—are overlaid with imperfect alignment. Derived from the French word for "watered silk", this optical phenomenon is not a feature of either original surface, but rather a "beat" or interference pattern formed by the way your eyes perceive light passing through the spaces. 

![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +4

Users on [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/explainlikeimfive/comments/hlpxvo/eli5_what_is_the_moir%C3%A9_effect/) agree that even the absolute smallest misalignment or rotation between identical grids can result in massive, easily visible shifts in the resulting wave or ripple effects. 

![](../assets/b8198132047e1c5b1c0d.png)Reddit

### How They Form

* **Geometric Obstruction:** When dark parts of the front pattern cover transparent parts of the rear layer, a dark section is created. Where the dark details completely overlap, the transparent areas line up and form a lighter section. 

  ![](../assets/9a841ae364110001b134.png)Exploratorium
* **The "Beat" Phenomenon:** Much like acoustic beats occur when two sound waves have slightly different frequencies, moiré fringes are spatial frequencies generated by a mismatch in spacing (pitch) or angle. 

  ![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +2

### Where Moiré Patterns Occur

* **Digital Media:** You often see these unwanted distortions when taking a photo or video of a television, computer monitor, or tightly patterned clothing (like tweed or pinstripes). The grid of the camera's sensor conflicts directly with the grid of pixels or fabric lines. 

  ![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +1
* **Everyday Life:** Moiré fringes naturally show up when looking through overlapping window screens, overlapping mesh curtains, or when passing a double-layered picket fence on a bridge. 

  ![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia +2
* **Modern Science ("Twistronics"):** Moiré configurations are revolutionizing materials science. When layers of 2D materials like **graphene** are stacked and twisted at precise "magic angles," the moiré landscape completely alters electron behaviors, unlocking exotic states like superconductivity. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Dr Manab

The connection between **moiré patterns and migraines** comes down to how your eyes and brain process repetitive lines, as well as the striking visual similarities between a moiré effect and a **migraine visual aura**.

### 1. The Visual Link: Scintillating Scotoma

When people experience a visual migraine (often called a [migraine with aura](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/migraine-with-aura/symptoms-causes/syc-20352072)), they commonly see an expanding, **shimmering, zigzag pattern** known as a [scintillating scotoma](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/scintillating-scotoma). These flickering, geometric lines can look remarkably like a vibrating or moving moiré pattern. 

![](../assets/a68518d4edde09b467a5.png)Mayo Clinic +1

* **The Cause:** Despite appearing in your "eye," typical visual auras actually start in the **brain's visual cortex**. A slow wave of electrical activity—called cortical spreading depression—moves across your brain cells, causing them to misfire and create these phantom, geometric illusions. 

  ![](../assets/8982df35cecd010f9f4f.png)New England Retina Associates +2
* **The Both-Eyes Test:** Because the issue originates in the brain rather than the eyeball, these shimmering patterns will usually **appear in both eyes**, even if you close or cover one. 

  ![](../assets/78fe53a4978635b1aaba.png)Eyes In Disguise Optometry +1

### 2. Moiré Patterns as Migraine Triggers

For individuals prone to migraines, looking at real-life moiré patterns or highly repetitive, high-contrast stripes can actively **trigger a migraine attack**. This phenomenon is tied to **pattern glare** and visual stress: 

![](../assets/6de70fc4740c19b65ec6.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov)

* When your eyes stare at tightly packed, overlapping parallel lines or grids, it forces the neurons in your visual cortex to hyper-synchronize and over-activate. 

  ![](../assets/6de70fc4740c19b65ec6.png)National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov)
* In a migraine-prone brain, this sudden overload of electrical activity can overwhelm the visual system and spark the chemical cascade that leads to a headache. 

  ![](../assets/a68518d4edde09b467a5.png)Mayo Clinic +1

### ⚠️ When to Screen Your Symptoms

Medical experts distinguish between a standard brain-based aura and a **retinal migraine** (or ocular migraine), which is much rarer and stems from a temporary restriction of blood flow to a single eye. 

![](../assets/5718c8a3490f8b7688e7.png)nhs.uk +1

| Feature | Typical Migraine Aura | Retinal (Ocular) Migraine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eyes Affected** | **Both eyes** simultaneously | **One eye** only |
| **Primary Vision Change** | Shimmering zigzags, jagged "fortification" arcs, moving shapes | Sudden blind spots, blurring, or temporary blackouts/blindness |
| **Origin** | Visual cortex of the **brain** | Blood vessels inside the **eyeball** |
| **Urgency** | Generally benign; resolves in 20–60 mins | **Requires immediate medical check** to rule out stroke or retinal issues |
