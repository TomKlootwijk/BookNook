## Response 1 — Google AI Mode

In 19th-century Dutch politics, **Guillaume Groen van Prinsterer** was closely aligned with the **Orangists** (supporters of the House of Orange-Nassau). However, as the founder of the anti-revolutionary movement, his alliance was rooted more in historical and religious principles than blind loyalty to the crown. 

![](../assets/439853ee2c5403487075.png)Radboud Repository +3

Groen van Prinsterer’s relationship with Orangism is characterized by three main components:

### 1. The Archivist of the House of Orange

Before entering parliament, Groen served as the royal archivist for King William I. During this time, he edited the highly regarded *Archives et correspondance de la maison d'Orange*. Immersing himself in primary historical documents heavily shaped his worldview. He grew to see the House of Orange not just as political rulers, but as the providential protectors of Dutch independence and the Protestant faith. 

![](../assets/bd2aaf9b0d1846ccb55f.png)Rijksuniversiteit Groningen +5

### 2. Rewriting History from an Orangist Perspective

Groen modernized and revived the Orangist interpretation of Dutch history in his influential work, *Handboek der geschiedenis van het Vaderland*. 

![](../assets/c6558b8b044f9026e569.png)Project MUSE +1

* **The "Villains":** In his historical writings, he recast anti-Orangist factions—such as the 17th-century States Party (*Staatsgezinden*) and the 18th-century Patriots (*Patriotten*)—as the antagonists who weakened the nation. 

  ![](../assets/f48ec470e852199e1b30.png)Wikipedia
* **The "Sins" of the Patriots:** He argued that the Patriots committed a grave error in 1795 by welcoming French revolutionary forces to oust the Prince of Orange, which ultimately led to the loss of Dutch sovereignty. 

  ![](../assets/c6558b8b044f9026e569.png)Project MUSE

### 3. "Rather an Orangist than a Monarchist"

Despite his romantic view of the House of Orange, Groen was not a submissive royalist. His famous description was that he was **"rather an Orangist than a monarchist."** 

![](../assets/439853ee2c5403487075.png)Radboud Repository

He routinely clashed with the absolute power of King William I and King William II. Groen believed the monarch's authority was strictly subordinate to God's law. He championed a "historical-constitutional" path, attempting to reconcile the traditional sovereignty of the prince with the developing parliamentary system. He strongly opposed both the secular liberalism of the 1848 constitution and any attempts at Napoleonic, autocratic rule by the kings themselves. 

![](../assets/53ba11c4487352305229.png)Parlement.com +4

For Groen, supporting the Orangists was the ultimate vehicle for protecting what he called the "Christian-historical" identity of the Netherlands against the secularizing wave of the French Revolution. 

![](../assets/224a8bf88fe4c88baa95.jpg)Biografie Groen van Prinsterer
