That is a very creative and vivid way to visualize what happens to the body, but medically and chemically, death and cellular breakdown work quite differently. 
Here is how the science actually connects the concepts you mentioned: 
  • Oxygen doesn't "dye" bonds—it stops fueling them: When you die, your body stops taking in oxygen. Without oxygen, your cells can no longer produce ATP (adenine triphosphate), which is the universal energy currency of life. 
  • Why structure is lost: Without ATP energy, the microscopic pumps in your cell membranes fail. Calcium rushes into the cells, triggering enzymes that literally start digesting the cell from the inside out. This process is called autolysis, and it is the true cause of catastrophic structural loss, not a reaction with light or melanin. 
  • Melanin vs. Thermochromic Pigments: Melanin gives our skin, hair, and eyes their color by absorbing harmful ultraviolet (UV) light, but it is not a thermochromic pigment (pigments that change color based on temperature, like a mood ring). Melanin is highly stable and does not "rust" or break down easily from a lack of heat; in fact, melanin lasts so long that scientists can still find it intact in millions-of-years-old fossils. 
  • Light and Temperature: Living tissues don't rely on light hitting melanin to maintain their temperature. Our body heat is generated internally by our metabolism (burning calories). When metabolism stops at death, the body simply cools down to match the surrounding room temperature, a process called algor mortis. 
In short, structure is lost because the biochemical "power grid" shuts down, causing the cells to self-destruct, rather than a failure of light, heat, or melanin crystals.
