Modern military submarines use some of the most advanced and high-tech equipment in the world to navigate, communicate, and stay hidden underwater. 

![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Submarine Insider +1

### Navigation and Steering

* **Fiber-Optic Gyroscopes:** Because GPS signals cannot penetrate seawater, submarines use quantum physics and light beams sent through a fiber-optic coil to track tiny changes in direction. 

  ![](../assets/b64f829a692513b4962f.png)Instagram·Veritasium
* **Quartz Crystal Speedometers:** They measure speed using a tiny quartz crystal that changes pitch as water pressure and inertia shift when the sub accelerates. 

  ![](../assets/b64f829a692513b4962f.png)Instagram·Veritasium

### Stealth and Sound Control

* **Acoustic Tiles:** Hulls are covered in tens of thousands of specialized rubber or synthetic tiles designed to absorb active sonar waves and muffle internal noises. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Submarine Insider
* **Pump-Jet Propulsion:** Many modern boats replace traditional spinning propellers with pump-jet systems, which pull water in and push it out through a nozzle to dramatically reduce noise. 

  ![](../assets/9a05b6f8eb450be9efa3.png)YouTube·Submarine Insider

### Life Support and Power

* **Water Electrolysis:** Submarines purify seawater and pass an electric current through it to split water molecules, generating fresh, breathable oxygen for the crew without needing to surface.
* **Nuclear Reactors:** Advanced reactor cores can power a submarine for up to 20 to 42 years without ever needing to refuel.

## Response 3 — Google AI Mode

Privacy is virtually non-existent on a submarine. Space is so tightly rationed that crew members have to live in incredibly close quarters for months at a time, leading to a very unique, highly communal shipboard culture where personal boundaries are completely redefined.

The reality of living in these cramped conditions involves several unique challenges:

* **The "Cozy" Bunk Layout:** Standard bunks (often called "racks") are roughly the size of a small coffin. They are stacked three high, leaving only a few inches of clearance above your face.
* **The "Coffin Locker":** Your entire storage space for a six-month deployment is a tiny tray built directly underneath your mattress. It is only a few inches deep.
* **Hot-Bunking Realities:** Because space is at such a premium, two or three sailors assigned to different watch shifts will rotate in and out of the exact same bed. When one wakes up to go on shift, the next slides into a bed that is still warm.
* **Zero Personal Space:** Corridors are so narrow that two people passing each other have to turn sideways. Showers and bathrooms (heads) are shared by dozens of crew members, meaning lines are long and quick turnaround times are strictly enforced.

Because you are trapped in a steel tube with over a hundred people, the crew quickly learns to develop a high tolerance for ambient noise, lack of privacy, and constant close contact.
