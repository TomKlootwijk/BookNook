Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein found in red blood cells that carries oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body. 
Sanquin

Function and Normal Levels

  • Oxygen transport: It binds to oxygen in the lungs and releases it into body tissues. 
    Wikipedia +1
  • Carbon dioxide removal: It carries some carbon dioxide back to the lungs for breathing out. 
    Anadolu Medical Center +1
  • Normal ranges:
    • For adult males: roughly 13.5 to 17.5 g/dL (or 8.5 to 11 mmol/L).
    • For adult females: roughly 12.0 to 15.5 g/dL (or 7.5 to 10 mmol/L). 
      Cleveland Clinic +2

High and Low Levels

  • Low hemoglobin: Often called anemia, this causes fatigue, weakness, and dizziness because tissues get less oxygen. It can stem from iron deficiency, blood loss, or other health issues.
  • High hemoglobin: Known as erythrocytosis, this thickens the blood and can result from dehydration, smoking, high altitudes, or bone marrow conditions. 
    Mayo Clinic +4
