### 🏭 Chemical & Munitions Base: Napalm-B

The terms **orange acid**, **fatty lipids**, and **2 stage aerosol ignition** relate to the formulation and mechanics of incendiary devices and **Fuel-Air Explosives (FAE)**.

* **Orange Acid / Fatty Lipids:** Traditional napalm was made by gelling gasoline with aluminum salts of napalmic and palmitic acids (**fatty lipids**). During the Vietnam War, this was largely replaced by **Napalm-B**, which used polystyrene dissolved in benzene and gasoline. In tactical military slang and chemical supply frameworks, the corrosive thickeners and organic acid catalysts were occasionally color-coded or referred to by their chemical component streams (e.g., orange/brown industrial coding for acids and oils).

* **2-Stage Aerosol Ignition:** This refers to the operational mechanism of **thermobaric or Fuel-Air Explosives (FAEs)**, such as the CBU-55, which were heavily deployed in Vietnam. FAEs work in two distinct stages:
  1. A primary charge disperses a liquid fuel into a massive **aerosol cloud** that mixes with environmental oxygen.
  2. A secondary delayed detonator sparks the **ignition** of the cloud, producing an incredibly high-impulse blast wave over a broad area.

The phrases **lane bombing** and **"tower of hanoi strategy"** tie back to the strategic bombing campaigns over North Vietnam, specifically targeting the transportation corridors and heavily fortified zones around Hanoi.

* **Lane Bombing:** This refers to tactical routing protocols where bombers were funneled into structured flight paths or "lanes" to maximize saturation over dense jungle pathways like the Ho Chi Minh Trail or to safely clear established anti-aircraft boundaries.
* **"Tower of Hanoi Strategy":** This is a conceptual application of the mathematical [Tower of Hanoi puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Hanoi) to military logistics and operational scheduling. In defense logistics and continuous operations, a "Tower of Hanoi" schedule is used as an optimal rotation framework—frequently deployed in digital data backup schemes to rotate media efficiently. In the context of the Vietnam War air campaigns (such as *Operation Linebacker I and II*), it mapped out waves of bomber groups. By staggering the deployment of specific carrier wings based on their proximity, maintenance cycles, and target distance, planners ensured a constant, rolling sequence of strikes over Hanoi without exhausting deployment limits all at once.
