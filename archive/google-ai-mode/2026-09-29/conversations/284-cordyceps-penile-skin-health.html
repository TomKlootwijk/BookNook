If you are noticing unusual spots, bumps, or texturing on the head of your penis, it is highly likely you are thinking of Fordyce spots or Pearly Penile Papules (PPPs) rather than Cordyceps, which is a type of parasitic fungus. 
Riverchase Dermatology +1
  • Fordyce spots are completely harmless, visible sebaceous (oil) glands. They present as tiny, painless, flesh-colored, yellow, or white bumps that often appear on the shaft or head of the penis, as well as the scrotum or lips. They are non-contagious and require no treatment. 
    Riverchase Dermatology
  • Pearly penile papules (PPPs) are small, benign, dome-shaped growths that typically form in one or several rows around the corona (the rounded border) of the penis head. Like Fordyce spots, they are harmless, not sexually transmitted, and do not cause health issues. 
    YouTube·Jason Emer, MD
Cordyceps, on the other hand, is a medicinal mushroom known in traditional Asian medicine. While it is sometimes marketed in oral supplements aimed at male vitality, athletic stamina, or erectile support, it does not grow on humans and cannot infect human skin or genitalia. 
National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov) +1

Identifying the Cause

Because various conditions can cause spots or changes on the penis head, it is important to pay attention to your symptoms to determine if you need to see a doctor: 
  • Infections: If the bumps are painful, itchy, oozing, spreading, or blistered, they could indicate a sexually transmitted infection (STI) like herpes or genital warts, or a fungal yeast infection (balanitis). 
  • Normal Anatomy: If the bumps are painless, have been there a long time without changing, and don't cause irritation, they are likely Fordyce spots or PPPs. 
    Riverchase Dermatology +1
Please avoid applying any home remedies, herbal extracts, or harsh over-the-counter creams directly to the head of your penis, as this sensitive tissue easily becomes irritated, burned, or scarred. If you are experiencing pain, itching, discharge, or are simply unsure of what the bumps are, schedule a brief physical evaluation with a healthcare provider or urologist for a proper diagnosis.

No, the "corona" of the penis is not related to a virus. The word "corona" simply means "crown" in Latin. In anatomy, the corona of the glans is the rounded, raised border at the base of the penis head. When to Seek Medical Attention

Stop applying the cream and consult a healthcare provider or a local pharmacist if you experience any of the following: 
  • Your symptoms (redness, itching, burning, or white discharge) do not begin to improve after 7 days of consistent treatment.
  • You experience severe burning, blisters, or open sores after applying the cream.
  • You develop a fever, swollen groin lymph nodes, or a foul-smelling discharge, which could indicate a bacterial infection or a sexually transmitted infection (STI).
