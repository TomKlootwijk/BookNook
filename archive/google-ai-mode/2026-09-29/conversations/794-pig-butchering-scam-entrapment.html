In this scenario, if someone took your money—even if it seemed like a small amount or "pennies on the dollar"—and refused to return any of it, they have committed fraud and theft. 
In the context of investment or trust scams, this is a calculated psychological tactic known as the commitment trap. 
Here is exactly how this mechanic functions in a fraudulent scheme: 

🎣 The Mechanics of the "Pennies on the Dollar" Trap

  • Low Barrier to Entry: Scammers often start by asking for a very small amount of money (the "pennies"). They do this because it lowers your guard and makes the risk feel negligible. 
  • The Illusion of Success: On their fake apps or websites, they will show that your small amount of money has magically doubled or tripled. 
  • The Withdrawal Bait: They may even let you successfully withdraw a tiny amount of cash early on. This is a deliberate ploy to prove the system is "real" and build absolute trust. 
  • The Scale-Up: Once you believe the system works, they convince you to deposit significantly larger sums (thousands or tens of thousands of dollars). 
  • The Lockdown: The moment you try to withdraw your main funds, the trap snaps shut. They stop returning your money, ghost you, or demand exorbitant "taxes" or "fees" to unlock the account. 

🛑 The Bottom Line

If a platform or individual takes any amount of your money and refuses to let you withdraw it without paying more money first, it is a scam. 
Legitimate financial platforms do not hold your principal investment hostage or demand upfront cash fees to process a withdrawal. Any money sent to them is already gone, and sending more money will not get it back.
