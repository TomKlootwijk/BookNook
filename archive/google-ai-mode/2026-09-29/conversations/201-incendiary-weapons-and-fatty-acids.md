### 1. The Fatty Acids that Ignite: Napalm

If you are thinking of a substance dropped during a bombing run that is formulated from fatty acids, you are looking for **[Napalm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napalm)**.

* **The Formulation:** The name "Napalm" is a literal portmanteau of its original thickening agents: **na**phthenic acid and **palm**itic acid (a fatty acid).
* **How it works:** These aluminum salts of fatty acids act as a gelling agent when mixed with volatile petrochemicals like gasoline or diesel. This creates a sticky, highly flammable gel that adheres to targets and burns at intense temperatures (800–1,200 °C).

### 2. Two-Stage Hydrocarbon Ignition

The mention of "ignite 2-stage" refers to a thermodynamic and chemical kinetic phenomenon known as **two-stage ignition**.

* When heavy hydrocarbon fuels (including biodiesel made of **fatty acid methyl esters**) undergo combustion, they don't ignite all at once.
* **Stage 1 (Cool Flame):** Low-temperature chemical reactions begin, breaking down the fatty acid chains and releasing intermediate molecules like formaldehyde and hydrogen peroxide, resulting in a mild temperature rise.
* **Stage 2 (Hot Ignition):** Once a critical temperature threshold is crossed, a secondary, highly explosive main heat release triggers, completely igniting the remaining fuel.

### 3. Agent Orange (The Clarification)

**[Agent Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange)** was **not an incendiary weapon** and did not ignite targets. It was a chemical herbicide and defoliant used by the U.S. military during the Vietnam War to clear forest canopy and destroy crops. It consisted of an equal mixture of two synthetic herbicides (2,4-D and 2,4,5-T) and was notoriously contaminated with a toxic dioxin (TCDD). Because TCDD is **fat-soluble**, it accumulates heavily in human and animal fatty tissues, causing severe multi-generational health defects and cancers rather than physical burns.
