⚠️ Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Emergency Care)

You should go to an emergency room or seek urgent medical care immediately if you experience any of the following: 
  • A prolonged erection: An erection that lasts for more than 4 hours or is unrelated to sexual desire (priapism) is a medical emergency that requires swift treatment to prevent permanent damage.

  • A "popping" sound or sudden severe trauma: If the pain started suddenly during sexual activity with a popping sound, severe swelling, or deep bruising, it could indicate a penile fracture, which requires emergency surgery. 
    MedicalNewsToday +1
  • Inability to urinate: A complete blockage or inability to pass urine. 
  • Severe, spreading pain: Pain accompanied by a high fever, chills, nausea, or severe pain spreading to your lower abdomen or groin.

Common Causes of Pain

If you are not experiencing emergency symptoms, the discomfort could be related to one of several common conditions: 
  • Infections: Urinary tract infections (UTIs) or sexually transmitted infections (STIs like chlamydia, gonorrhea, or herpes) can cause a burning sensation, especially during urination, as well as discharge or sores.
  • Skin or Foreskin Inflammation: Conditions like balanitis (inflammation of the head of the penis or foreskin) can cause redness, itching, and throbbing pain, often due to irritation or fungal/bacterial overgrowth.
  • Minor Trauma or Friction: Strenuous sexual activity, lack of lubrication, or minor sports injuries can cause temporary soreness.
  • Structural Issues: Conditions like Peyronie's disease cause scar tissue to build up inside the penis, leading to a painful curvature during an erection. 
    MyHealth Alberta +5

What You Can Do Right Now

  • Rest: Avoid any sexual activity or vigorous physical movement that could further irritate the area.
  • Gentle Hygiene: Wash the area gently with warm water only. Avoid harsh soaps, body washes, or chemical lotions that can worsen inflammation.
  • Cool Compress: If there is mild swelling or surface soreness, you can apply a cold cloth or an ice pack wrapped securely in a towel for 10–15 minutes. Never apply ice directly to the skin.
  • See a Doctor: Schedule an appointment with a primary care physician or a urologist to get an accurate diagnosis and appropriate treatment (such as targeted antibiotics or creams).
