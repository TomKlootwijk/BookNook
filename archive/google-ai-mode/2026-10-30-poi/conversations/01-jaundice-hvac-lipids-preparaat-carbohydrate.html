The clear physiological link between these terms involves bilirubin metabolism and the liver's processing of macronutrients. Here is a quick breakdown of how these specific concepts interconnect: 

1. Jaundice & Lipids

Jaundice (icterus) is the yellow discoloration of the skin and sclera caused by elevated blood levels of bilirubin (>2–3 mg/dL). 
AASLD +1
  • The Lipid Connection: Bilirubin starts as unconjugated bilirubin (UCB), which is highly lipid-soluble (hydrophobic). Because it cannot dissolve in water, it must bind to albumin in the bloodstream to travel safely to the liver. 
    YouTube·Osmosis from Elsevier +1
  • Furthermore, the liver secretes conjugated bilirubin into bile, whose primary physical function is to emulsify dietary lipids to facilitate digestion and fat absorption in the small intestine. 
    National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov)

2. Carbohydrate & The Liver

The liver converts lipid-soluble bilirubin into a water-soluble form via a carbohydrate-driven process called glucuronidation. Inside the liver cells (hepatocytes), UCB is chemically bonded to molecules of glucuronic acid (a sugar acid derived from glucose/carbohydrates) by the enzyme uridine diphosphate glucuronosyltransferase (UGT1A1). This converts it into conjugated bilirubin, which can then be safely excreted into bile and stool. 
YouTube·Armando Hasudungan +3

3. "Preparaat" (Preparation)

In clinical diagnostics and lab medicine (often referenced in Dutch medical literature as a preparaat or sample preparation), measuring these compounds requires specific preparation: 
NVKC
  • Total & Direct Bilirubin Tests: Blood samples are prepared to isolate conjugated (direct) vs. unconjugated (indirect) bilirubin to pinpoint whether jaundice is pre-hepatic (e.g., hemolytic anemia), hepatic (liver damage), or post-hepatic (bile duct obstruction). 
    YouTube·Medicosis Perfectionalis +1
  • Carbohydrate-Deficient Transferrin (CDT): Lab preparations of serum are also frequently used to measure carbohydrate variations in transferrin proteins to evaluate chronic alcohol consumption, a major cause of liver dysfunction and subsequent jaundice. 
    NVKC
